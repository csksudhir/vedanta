    ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ದ್ವಿತೀಯ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೫

    Praśna Upaniṣad — Second Question, Verse 5

    Source of Ṭīkā: Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

    ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

    ಏಷೋಽಗ್ನಿಸ್ತಪತ್ಯೇಷ ಸೂರ್ಯ ಏಷ ಪರ್ಜನ್ಯೋ ಮಘವಾನೇಷ ವಾಯುಃ ।

    ಏಷ ಪೃಥಿವೀ ರಯಿರ್ದೇವಃ ಸದಸಚ್ಚಾಮೃತಂ ಚ ಯತ್ ॥ ೫ ॥

    Overall Translation

    This one (Prāṇa) is Fire — it heats. This one is the Sun. This one is Parjanya (the rain-god). This one is Maghavan (Indra). This one is Vāyu (wind).

    This one is Earth, Rayi, the shining one — whatever is formed (sat) and formless (asat) and immortal (amṛta).

    Word-to-Word

    ಏಷಃ — this one (Prāṇa) · ಅಗ್ನಿಃ — Fire · ತಪತಿ — heats / shines · ಏಷಃ — this one · ಸೂರ್ಯಃ — the Sun · ಏಷಃ — this one · ಪರ್ಜನ್ಯಃ — Parjanya (the rain-god) · ಮಘವಾನ್ — Maghavan (Indra) · ಏಷಃ — this one · ವಾಯುಃ — Vāyu (wind) ।

    ಏಷಃ — this one · ಪೃಥಿವೀ — Earth · ರಯಿಃ — Rayi (the nourishing/material principle) · ದೇವಃ — the shining one / the divine · ಸತ್ — the formed / the manifest · ಅಸತ್ ಚ — and the formless / unmanifest · ಅಮೃತಂ ಚ — and the immortal · ಯತ್ — whatever ॥

    ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

    ಕಥಮ್ ? ಏಷಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಸನ್ ತಪತಿ ಜ್ವಲತಿ ।

    ತಥಾ ಏಷಃ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ಸನ್ ಪ್ರಕಾಶತೇ ।

    ತಥಾ ಏಷಃ ಪರ್ಜನ್ಯಃ ಸನ್ ವರ್ಷತಿ ।

    ಕಿಂಚ ಮಘವಾನ್ ಇಂದ್ರಃ ಸನ್ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪಾಲಯತಿ ಜಿಘಾಂಸತಿ ಅಸುರರಕ್ಷಾಂಸಿ ।

    ಕಿಂಚ ಏಷಃ ವಾಯುಃ ಆವಹ-ಪ್ರವಹ-ಆದಿ-ಭೇದಃ ।

    ಕಿಂಚ ಏಷಃ ಪೃಥಿವೀ ರಯಿಃ ದೇವಃ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ ಸತ್ ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಅಸತ್ ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ ಅಮೃತಂ ಚ ಯತ್ ದೇವಾನಾಂ ಸ್ಥಿತಿಕಾರಣಂ ॥

    Overall Translation

    How (is Prāṇa the foremost)? This Prāṇa — being Fire — heats, blazes. Similarly this one — being the Sun — shines. Similarly this one — being Parjanya — rains.

    Moreover, being Maghavan (Indra), it protects the creatures and desires to destroy the demons and rākṣasas.

    Moreover, this one is Vāyu — of the divisions called āvaha, pravaha and the like.

    Moreover, this one is Earth, Rayi, the shining one — whatever in all the world is formed (sat / gross) and formless (asat / subtle) — and what is immortal, the cause of the stability of the gods.

    Word-to-Word

    ಕಥಮ್ — how? · ಏಷಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — this Prāṇa · ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಸನ್ — being Fire · ತಪತಿ — heats · ಜ್ವಲತಿ — blazes ।

    ತಥಾ — similarly · ಏಷಃ — this one · ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ಸನ್ — being the Sun · ಪ್ರಕಾಶತೇ — shines ।

    ತಥಾ — similarly · ಏಷಃ — this one · ಪರ್ಜನ್ಯಃ ಸನ್ — being Parjanya · ವರ್ಷತಿ — rains ।

    ಕಿಂಚ — moreover · ಮಘವಾನ್ ಇಂದ್ರಃ ಸನ್ — being Maghavan (Indra) · ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪಾಲಯತಿ — protects the creatures · ಜಿಘಾಂಸತಿ — desires to destroy · ಅಸುರರಕ್ಷಾಂಸಿ — the demons and rākṣasas ।

    ಕಿಂಚ — moreover · ಏಷಃ — this one · ವಾಯುಃ — Vāyu · ಆವಹ-ಪ್ರವಹ-ಆದಿ-ಭೇದಃ — of the divisions called āvaha, pravaha, and the like ।

    ಕಿಂಚ — moreover · ಏಷಃ — this one · ಪೃಥಿವೀ — Earth · ರಯಿಃ — Rayi · ದೇವಃ — the shining one · ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ — of all the world · ಸತ್ — the formed / gross · ಮೂರ್ತಂ — having form · ಅಸತ್ — the formless · ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ — and without form · ಅಮೃತಂ ಚ — and the immortal · ಯತ್ — whatever · ದೇವಾನಾಂ — of the gods · ಸ್ಥಿತಿಕಾರಣಂ — the cause of stability ॥

    Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

    The final statement of the Bhāṣya accumulates five predicates of Prāṇa in one sentence and requires explicit anvaya.

    Sentence — Prāṇa as Earth, Rayi, deva, and the cause of the gods' stability

    As written:

    ಏಷಃ ಪೃಥಿವೀ ರಯಿಃ ದೇವಃ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ ಸತ್ ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಅಸತ್ ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ ಅಮೃತಂ ಚ ಯತ್ ದೇವಾನಾಂ ಸ್ಥಿತಿಕಾರಣಂ ।

    Anvaya (resolved prose order):

    ಏಷಃ [ಪ್ರಾಣಃ] — [೧] ಪೃಥಿವೀ [ಸನ್ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ ಧಾರಯಿತಾ], [೨] ರಯಿಃ [= ಚಂದ್ರಃ ಸನ್ ಸರ್ವಂ ಪುಷ್ಣಾತಿ], [೩] ದೇವಃ [ಪ್ರಕಾಶಮಾನಃ], [೪] ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ ಯತ್ ಸತ್ ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ ಅಸತ್ ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ [ತತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ ಏಷಃ ಏವ], [೫] ಅಮೃತಂ ಯತ್ ದೇವಾನಾಂ ಸ್ಥಿತಿಕಾರಣಂ [ತತ್ ಅಪಿ ಏಷಃ ಏವ] ।

    English: This Prāṇa is [1] Earth — being Earth it sustains all the world; [2] Rayi — being the Moon it nourishes all; [3] the shining deva; [4] whatever in all the world is formed (gross) and formless (subtle) — that too is this alone; [5] and the immortal, which is the cause of the stability of the gods — that too is this alone.

    Note: The sentence lists five cosmic identifications of Prāṇa. Ānandagiri's gloss separates Earth (dharaṇa = sustaining) from Rayi/Moon (poṣaṇa = nourishing), showing they are two distinct aspects though both refer to Prāṇa. The "amṛtam" (immortal) is the cause of the gods' stability — it is distinct from the formed/formless pair and points to Prāṇa's transcendent aspect.

    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

    ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ಸನ್ ಇತಿ ।

    ತಪತಿ ಇತಿ ಅನುಷಂಗಃ ಸಂತಾಪಯತಿ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ಆವಹ-ಪ್ರವಹ-ಆದಿ ಇತಿ ।

    ಆವಹ-ಆದಯಃ ಸಪ್ತ ವಾಯು-ಗುಣಾಃ ತಥಾಭೂತಃ ಸನ್ ಮೇಘಾನ್ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಶ್ಚಕ್ರ-ಆದೀನ್ ಚ ವಹತಿ ಇತಿ ಶೇಷಃ ।

    ಏಷ ದೇವಃ ಪೃಥಿವೀ ಸನ್ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ ಧಾರಯಿತಾ ರಯಿಃ ಚಂದ್ರಃ ಸನ್ ಸರ್ವಂ ಪುಷ್ಣಾತಿ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ॥ ೫ ॥

    Overall Translation

    On "sūryaḥ san" (being the Sun): Ānandagiri notes that the word "tapati" (heats) carries over by anuṣaṅga (syntactic carryover) — "being the Sun, it heats / warms (all beings)" is the full meaning.

    On "āvaha-pravaha-ādi" (āvaha, pravaha and the like): Ānandagiri explains that āvaha and the rest are the seven qualities / divisions of Vāyu. Being of that nature, the implied completion of the sentence (śeṣa) is: "it carries the clouds, the wheel of luminaries and the like." The seven winds — āvaha, pravaha, udvaha, vivaha, parivaha, parāvaha, and suṣumṇā — each carry their specific cosmic functions.

    On the final statement about Earth and Rayi: Ānandagiri clarifies — "this deva, being Earth, is the sustainer (dhārayitā) of all the world; and being Rayi — the Moon — it nourishes (puṣṇāti) all." Thus Earth and Rayi/Moon are two distinct roles of the one Prāṇa: Earth sustains by upholding, Moon nourishes by providing food-sap (rasa).

    Word-to-Word

    ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ಸನ್ ಇತಿ — on the phrase "sūryaḥ san (being the Sun)" ।

    ತಪತಿ ಇತಿ ಅನುಷಂಗಃ — the word "tapati (heats)" carries over by anuṣaṅga · ಸಂತಾಪಯತಿ — heats / warms (all beings) · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

    ಆವಹ-ಪ್ರವಹ-ಆದಿ ಇತಿ — on the phrase "āvaha, pravaha and the like" ।

    ಆವಹ-ಆದಯಃ — āvaha and the rest · ಸಪ್ತ ವಾಯು-ಗುಣಾಃ — the seven qualities / divisions of Vāyu · ತಥಾಭೂತಃ ಸನ್ — being of that nature · ಮೇಘಾನ್ — the clouds · ಜ್ಯೋತಿಶ್ಚಕ್ರ-ಆದೀನ್ ಚ — and the wheel of luminaries and the like · ವಹತಿ — carries · ಇತಿ ಶೇಷಃ — this is the implied completion of the sentence ।

    ಏಷ ದೇವಃ — this deva · ಪೃಥಿವೀ ಸನ್ — being Earth · ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಜಗತಃ — of all the world · ಧಾರಯಿತಾ — the sustainer · ರಯಿಃ — Rayi · ಚಂದ್ರಃ ಸನ್ — being the Moon · ಸರ್ವಂ — all · ಪುಷ್ಣಾತಿ — nourishes · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ॥

    Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

    Text Author Function
    ಮೂಲ (Mūla) Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) The praise of Prāṇa — it is identified with Fire, Sun, Parjanya, Indra, Vāyu, Earth, Rayi, and the immortal; this is the śloka cited to answer "who is the foremost?"
    ಭಾṣya (Bhāṣya) Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) Explains each identification — Prāṇa as Fire heats, as Sun shines, as Parjanya rains, as Indra protects and destroys demons, as Vāyu moves in seven divisions, as Earth sustains, as Rayi nourishes, and as the immortal is the cause of gods' stability
    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā) Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) Clarifies anuṣaṅga of "tapati" for the Sun; names the seven divisions of Vāyu (āvaha etc.) and supplies the implied predicate; distinguishes Earth as dhārayitā (sustainer) from Rayi/Moon as poṣṭā (nourisher)

    Core Philosophical Thread

    1. Prāṇa is the subject of all cosmic functions — fire, rain, sun, wind, earth, moon are all its manifestations. The praise establishes Prāṇa as the one reality underlying all the gods named in verse 2.
    2. The seven Vāyus — āvaha, pravaha, udvaha, vivaha, parivaha, parāvaha, suṣumṇā — represent the cosmic functions of wind, each carrying a specific cosmic load (clouds, luminaries etc.).
    3. Sat and asat (formed and formless) — Prāṇa encompasses both gross matter and subtle matter. This directly echoes the first Praśna's Rayi analysis where both mūrta and amūrta were Rayi.
    4. Amṛtam (the immortal) — the cause of the gods' stability. This is Prāṇa in its transcendent, deathless aspect — beyond both sat and asat.
    5. Earth (dhāraṇa) vs Rayi/Moon (poṣaṇa) — Ānandagiri's distinction is precise: the Earth-aspect of Prāṇa sustains the world by upholding it; the Rayi/Moon-aspect nourishes it by providing the food-sap that feeds all living beings.
