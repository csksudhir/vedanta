    ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೬

    Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 6

    Source of Ṭīkā: Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

    ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

    ಅಥಾದಿತ್ಯ ಉದಯನ್ಯತ್ಪ್ರಾಚೀಂ ದಿಶಂ ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ ತೇನ ಪ್ರಾಚ್ಯಾನ್ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ರಶ್ಮಿಷು ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ ।

    ಯದ್ದಕ್ಷಿಣಾಂ ಯತ್ಪ್ರತೀಚೀಂ ಯದುದೀಚೀಂ ಯದಧೋ ಯದೂರ್ಧ್ವಂ ಯದಂತರಾ ದಿಶೋ ಯತ್ಸರ್ವಂ, ಪ್ರಕಾಶಯತಿ ತೇನ, ಸರ್ವಾನ್ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ರಶ್ಮಿಷು ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ ॥ ೬ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Now, when the Sun (Āditya) rises and enters the Eastern direction — by that, he gathers the prāṇas of the East into his rays.

    Whatever he illumines — the South, the West, the North, below, above, the intermediate directions, and everything — by that, he gathers all the prāṇas into his rays.

    Word-to-Word

    ಅಥ — now / then · ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ — the Sun (Āditya) · ಉದಯನ್ — rising · ಯತ್ — which · ಪ್ರಾಚೀಂ ದಿಶಂ — the Eastern direction · ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ — enters / pervades · ತೇನ — by that · ಪ್ರಾಚ್ಯಾನ್ — of the East · ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ — the prāṇas (living beings) · ರಶ್ಮಿಷು — in (his) rays · ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ — gathers / places together ।

    ಯತ್ — whatever · ದಕ್ಷಿಣಾಂ — the South · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಪ್ರತೀಚೀಂ — the West · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಉದೀಚೀಂ — the North · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಅಧಃ — below · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಊರ್ಧ್ವಂ — above · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಅಂತರಾ ದಿಶಃ — intermediate directions · ಯತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ — whatever everything · ಪ್ರಕಾಶಯತಿ — he illumines · ತೇನ — by that · ಸರ್ವಾನ್ — all · ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ — the prāṇas (living beings) · ರಶ್ಮಿಷು — in (his) rays · ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ — he gathers ॥

    ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

    ತಥಾ ಅಮೂರ್ತಃ ಅಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ ಯತ್ ಚ ಅದ್ಯಂ ।

    ಕಥಮ್ ? ಅಥ ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ ಉದಯನ್ ಉದ್ಗಚ್ಛನ್ ಪ್ರಾಣಿನಾಂ ಚಕ್ಷುರ್ಗೋಚರಮಾಗಚ್ಛನ್ ಯತ್ ಪ್ರಾಚೀಂ ದಿಶಂ ಸ್ವಪ್ರಕಾಶೇನ ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ ವ್ಯಾಪ್ನೋತಿ, ತೇನ ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮವ್ಯಾಪ್ತ್ಯಾ ಸರ್ವಾಂತಃಸ್ಥಾನ್ ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ ಪ್ರಾಚ್ಯಾನ್ ಅನ್ನಭೂತಾನ್ ರಶ್ಮಿಷು ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಾವಭಾಸರೂಪೇಷು ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತಿಮತ್ಸು ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತತ್ವಾತ್ ಪ್ರಾಣಿನಃ ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ ಸಂನಿವೇಶಯತಿ ಆತ್ಮಭೂತಾನ್ ಕರೋತಿ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ತಥೈವ ಯತ್ ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ ದಕ್ಷಿಣಾಂ ಯತ್ ಪ್ರತೀಚೀಂ ಯತ್ ಉದೀಚೀಮ್ ಅಧಃ ಊರ್ಧ್ವಂ ಯತ್ ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ ಯತ್ ಚ ಅಂತರಾ ದಿಶಃ ಕೋಣದಿಶಃ ಅವಾಂತರದಿಶಃ ಯತ್ ಚ ಅನ್ಯತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ ಪ್ರಕಾಶಯತಿ, ತೇನ ಸ್ವಪ್ರಕಾಶವ್ಯಾಪ್ತ್ಯಾ ಸರ್ವಾನ್ ಸರ್ವದಿಕ್ಸ್ಥಾನ್ ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ ರಶ್ಮಿಷು ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Similarly, Prāṇa — even though formless (amūrta) — is the eater, and is everything that exists, and also everything that is eaten.

    How? When the Sun (Āditya) rises — ascending, coming within the range of the eye of living beings — whatever Eastern direction he enters and pervades with his own light: by that — by the pervasion of his own Self — he gathers the prāṇas of the East (those who are within all things, those who have become food) into his rays, which are of the nature of the illumination of his own Self, which are pervaded (by him). He places them within himself, making them his own Self — this is the meaning.

    Similarly, whatever he enters of the South, West, North, below, above — and the intermediate directions (the corner directions, the sub-directions) — and whatever else, all of it, he illumines: by that — by the pervasion of his own light — he gathers all the prāṇas of all directions into his rays.

    Word-to-Word

    ತಥಾ — similarly · ಅಮೂರ್ತಃ ಅಪಿ — even though formless · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — Prāṇa · ಅತ್ತಾ — the eater · ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ — everything indeed · ಯತ್ ಚ ಅದ್ಯಂ — and also whatever is eaten ।

    ಕಥಮ್ — how? · ಅಥ — now / then · ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ — the Sun · ಉದಯನ್ — rising · ಉದ್ಗಚ್ಛನ್ — ascending · ಪ್ರಾಣಿನಾಂ — of living beings · ಚಕ್ಷುರ್ಗೋಚರಂ — within the range of the eye · ಆಗಚ್ಛನ್ — coming / arriving · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಪ್ರಾಚೀಂ ದಿಶಂ — the Eastern direction · ಸ್ವಪ್ರಕಾಶೇನ — with his own light · ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ — enters · ವ್ಯಾಪ್ನೋತಿ — pervades · ತೇನ — by that · ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮ-ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತ್ಯಾ — by the pervasion of his own Self · ಸರ್ವಾಂತಃಸ್ಥಾನ್ — those situated within all things · ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ — the prāṇas / living beings · ಪ್ರಾಚ್ಯಾನ್ — of the East · ಅನ್ನಭೂತಾನ್ — those who have become food · ರಶ್ಮಿಷು — in his rays · ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮ-ಅವಭಾಸ-ರೂಪೇಷು — which are of the nature of the illumination of his own Self · ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತಿಮತ್ಸು — which are pervaded · ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತತ್ವಾತ್ — because of being pervaded · ಪ್ರಾಣಿನಃ — the living beings · ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ — gathers / places · ಸಂನಿವೇಶಯತಿ — causes to be placed · ಆತ್ಮಭೂತಾನ್ ಕರೋತಿ — makes them his own Self · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

    ತಥೈವ — similarly · ಯತ್ ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ — whatever he enters · ದಕ್ಷಿಣಾಂ — the South · ಯತ್ ಪ್ರತೀಚೀಂ — whatever the West · ಯತ್ ಉದೀಚೀಂ — whatever the North · ಅಧಃ — below · ಊರ್ಧ್ವಂ — above · ಯತ್ ಪ್ರವಿಶತಿ — whatever he enters · ಯತ್ ಚ ಅಂತರಾ ದಿಶಃ — and whatever the intermediate directions · ಕೋಣದಿಶಃ — the corner directions · ಅವಾಂತರದಿಶಃ — the sub-directions · ಯತ್ ಚ ಅನ್ಯತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ — and whatever else, everything · ಪ್ರಕಾಶಯತಿ — he illumines · ತೇನ — by that · ಸ್ವಪ್ರಕಾಶ-ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತ್ಯಾ — by the pervasion of his own light · ಸರ್ವಾನ್ — all · ಸರ್ವದಿಕ್ಸ್ಥಾನ್ — situated in all directions · ಪ್ರಾಣಾನ್ — the prāṇas · ರಶ್ಮಿಷು — in his rays · ಸಂನಿಧತ್ತೇ — gathers ॥

    Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

    The Bhāṣya opens with a compressed statement and then provides a detailed explanation. The opening sentence requires explicit anvaya.

    Sentence — The opening identification of formless Prāṇa as eater and everything

    As written:

    ತಥಾ ಅಮೂರ್ತಃ ಅಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ ಯತ್ ಚ ಅದ್ಯಂ ।

    Anvaya (resolved prose order):

    [ಯಥಾ ರಯಿಃ ಸರ್ವಂ ಮೂರ್ತಮ್ ಅದ್ಯಂ ಚ ಉಕ್ತಃ —] ತಥಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅಪಿ ಅಮೂರ್ತಃ ಸನ್ — [ಏಕತಃ] ಅತ್ತಾ [ಚ], [ಅನ್ಯತಃ] ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ [ಭವತಿ] — ಯತ್ ಚ ಅದ್ಯಂ [ತತ್ ಅಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಣ ಏವ] ।

    English: Just as Rayi (the material principle) was said to be everything formed and also what is eaten — similarly Prāṇa too, even though formless, is on one side the eater, and is on the other side everything — and even what is eaten is also Prāṇa.

    Note: This sentence resolves the apparent tension in the second Praśna: Prāṇa was said to be the eater (attā), but also said to be everything (sarva). Ānandagiri flags this as the core point of the Bhāṣya — both the eater and the eaten are Prāṇa, because Prāṇa is the Self of all (sarvātmaka). The word "adyam" (what is eaten) is key — Ānandagiri confirms it covers both the gross food and the formless Prāṇa-as-food.

    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

    ರಯಿಶಬ್ದಿತಸ್ಯ ಅನ್ನಸ್ಯ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿತ್ವಾರ್ಥಂ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಂ ಉಕ್ತ್ವಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಸ್ಯ ಅಪಿ ತದರ್ಥಂ ಏವ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಂ ಉಚ್ಯತೇ ಅಥ ಆದಿತ್ಯ ಇತಿ ವಾಕ್ಯೇನ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ತಥಾ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿನಾ ।

    ಯತ್ ಚ ಅದ್ಯಂ ತದಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅತಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅಪಿ ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ ಇತಿ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಕಃ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ಸ್ವಪ್ರಕಾಶೇನ ಇತಿ ।

    ಸ್ವಕೀಯ-ಪ್ರಕಾಶೇನ ಸ್ವ-ಪ್ರಭಯಾ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ಅಂತರ್ಭೂತಾನ್ ಇತಿ ।

    ಯದ್ಯಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಣಸ್ಯ ಅತ್ತೃತ್ವಂ ಉಕ್ತಂ ತಥಾಪಿ ರಯಿರ್ವಾ ಏತತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ ಇತ್ಯತ್ರ ಅಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ ಪ್ರಾಣಸ್ಯ ಅಪಿ ಗುಣಭಾವ-ವಿವಕ್ಷಯಾ ಅನ್ನತ್ವಂ ಉಕ್ತಂ ಇತಿ ತಥೋಕ್ತಂ ।

    ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮ-ಅಭಾವಭಾಸ-ರೂಪೇಷು ಇತಿ ।

    ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮ-ಪ್ರಭಾ-ರೂಪೇಷು ರಶ್ಮಿಷು ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ವ್ಯಾಪ್ತತ್ವಾತ್ ಇತಿ ।

    ಸಂಬದ್ಧತ್ವಾತ್ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ॥ ೬ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Ānandagiri opens by explaining the structural purpose of this verse in the second Praśna: having stated the all-Self-nature (sarvātmatva) of food (called Rayi) for the purpose of establishing its Prajāpati-nature, now the all-Self-nature of Prāṇa is also stated for the same purpose — by the sentence beginning "atha āditya" (now the Sun). This is what Śaṅkara is doing — with the words "tathā" and so on.

    On the phrase "yat cādyam" (and what is eaten): Ānandagiri states the implication — even what is eaten is Prāṇa; therefore the eater-Prāṇa is also everything; thus Prāṇa is of the nature of all (sarvātmaka).

    On "svaprakāśena" (with his own light): Ānandagiri clarifies this means "with his own radiance, with his own luminosity" — the light that is intrinsic to the Sun, not borrowed from elsewhere.

    On "antarbhūtān" (those who are within, absorbed into): Ānandagiri resolves a potential difficulty. Although the quality of being the eater (attṛtva) was stated for Prāṇa, in the statement "rāyirvā etat sarvam" (all this is Rayi) the food-nature (annatva) was stated even for the formless Prāṇa — with the intent of describing it as having a subordinate quality (guṇabhāva-vivakṣayā). Therefore it was described that way (as food/Rayi).

    On "svātmāvabhāsarūpeṣu" (rays which are of the nature of the illumination of his own Self): Ānandagiri clarifies this means "in the rays which are of the nature of his own Self's radiance" — the rays are not separate from the Sun but are the very luminosity of the Sun's Self.

    On "vyāptatvāt" (because of being pervaded): Ānandagiri glosses this as "sambaddhatvāt" — because of being connected to / associated with (the Sun through his rays).

    Word-to-Word

    ರಯಿಶಬ್ದಿತಸ್ಯ — of what is designated as Rayi · ಅನ್ನಸ್ಯ — of food · ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿತ್ವ-ಅರ್ಥಂ — for the purpose of establishing its Prajāpati-nature · ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಂ — the all-Self-nature · ಉಕ್ತ್ವಾ — having stated · ಪ್ರಾಣಸ್ಯ ಅಪಿ — of Prāṇa too · ತದರ್ಥಂ ಏವ — for that very purpose · ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಂ — the all-Self-nature · ಉಚ್ಯತೇ — is stated · ಅಥ ಆದಿತ್ಯ ಇತಿ ವಾಕ್ಯೇನ — by the sentence beginning "atha āditya (now the Sun)" · ಇತ್ಯಾಹ — thus states · ತಥಾ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿನಾ — with the words "tathā (similarly)" and so on ।

    Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

    Text Author Function
    ಮೂಲ (Mūla) Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) Describes the Sun entering and illumining all directions and gathering all prāṇas into his rays — a cosmological image of Prāṇa's universal pervasion
    ಭಾṣya (Bhāṣya) Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) Explains the verse as establishing the sarvātmatva (all-Self-nature) of Prāṇa; shows that even the formless Prāṇa is both the eater and everything that is eaten; interprets rays as the Sun's own Self-luminosity pervading all living beings
    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā) Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) Identifies the structural purpose — parallel to the Rayi's sarvātmatva, now Prāṇa's sarvātmatva is established; explains the subordinate food-nature of Prāṇa (guṇabhāva); clarifies svaprakāśa as intrinsic radiance and vyāptatvāt as sambaddhatva

    Core Philosophical Thread

    1. Structural parallel: The second Praśna establishes sarvātmatva for both Rayi and Prāṇa. Verse 6 does for Prāṇa what an earlier verse did for Rayi — shows it as the Self of all. Ānandagiri makes this explicit.
    2. Prāṇa is both eater and eaten — the eater (attā) and what is eaten (adyam) are both Prāṇa, because Prāṇa is sarvātmaka (the Self of all). This is the philosophical resolution of the apparent paradox stated at the start of the Bhāṣya.
    3. The rays are the Sun's own Self-luminosity — not external instruments but the very radiance of his Self. When the Sun pervades a direction through his rays, he is making that direction his own Self. ātmasātkurvan (making his own Self) from verse 7 is already anticipated here.
    4. Guṇabhāva-vivakṣā — Ānandagiri introduces this technical term to explain why Prāṇa (the formless) could be described as food (the formed) in an earlier verse. It was stated with the intent of describing a subordinate aspect — not Prāṇa's primary nature but a secondary characterisation for the purpose of the argument.
    5. Vyāptatvāt = sambaddhatvāt — Ānandagiri's gloss simplifies the compound: the prāṇas of living beings are gathered into the rays because they are connected to / pervaded by those rays. The Sun's daily rising is the concrete, observable form of this metaphysical pervasion.
