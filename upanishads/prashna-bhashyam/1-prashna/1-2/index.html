    ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೨

    Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 2

    Source of Ṭīkā: Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

    ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

    ತಾನ್ ಹ ಸ ಋಷಿರುವಾಚ ಭೂಯ ಏವ ತಪಸಾ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ ಸಂವತ್ಸರಂ ಸಂವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ ।

    ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಾನ್ ಪೃಚ್ಛತ ।

    ಯದಿ ವಿಜ್ಞಾಸ್ಯಾಮಃ ಸರ್ವಂ ಹ ವೋ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮ ಇತಿ ॥ ೨ ॥

    Overall Translation

    To them the sage said: "Dwell here for a year more — with austerity, brahmacarya, and faith.

    Then ask whatever questions you wish.

    If we shall know (the answer), we shall tell you all of it."

    Word-to-Word

    ತಾನ್ — to them · ಹ — indeed · ಸಃ — he · ಋಷಿಃ — the sage (Pippalāda) · ಉವಾಚ — said · ಭೂಯಃ ಏವ — once more / again · ತಪಸಾ — with austerity · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ — with brahmacarya (celibacy / disciplined conduct) · ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ — with faith · ಸಂವತ್ಸರಂ — for a year · ಸಂವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ — you shall dwell / live (here) ।

    ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ — as you wish · ಪ್ರಶ್ನಾನ್ — questions · ಪೃಚ್ಛತ — ask ।

    ಯದಿ — if · ವಿಜ್ಞಾಸ್ಯಾಮಃ — we shall know · ಸರ್ವಂ — all · ಹ — indeed · ವಃ — to you · ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮಃ — we shall tell · ಇತಿ — thus ॥

    ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

    ತಾನ್ ಏವಂ ಉಪಗತಾನ್ ಸಃ ಹ ಕಿಲ ಋಷಿಃ ಉವಾಚ ಭೂಯಃ ಪುನಃ ಏವ — ಯದ್ಯಪಿ ಯೂಯಂ ಪೂರ್ವಂ ತಪಸ್ವಿನಃ ಏವ, ತಥಾಪಿ ಇಹ ತಪಸಾ ಇಂದ್ರಿಯಸಂಯಮೇನ ।

    ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ ಚ ಆಸ್ತಿಕ್ಯಬುದ್ಧ್ಯಾ ಆದರವಂತಃ ಸಂವತ್ಸರಂ ಕಾಲಂ ಸಂವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ ಸಮ್ಯಕ್-ಗುರು-ಶುಶ್ರೂಷಾ-ಪರಾಃ ಸಂತಃ ವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ ।

    ತತಃ ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ ಯಃ ಯಸ್ಯ ಕಾಮಃ ತಮ್ ಅನತಿಕ್ರಮ್ಯ ಯದ್ವಿಷಯೇ ಯಸ್ಯ ಜಿಜ್ಞಾಸಾ ತದ್ವಿಷಯಾನ್ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಾನ್ ಪೃಚ್ಛತ ।

    ಯದಿ ತತ್ ಯುಷ್ಮತ್ ಪೃಷ್ಟಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾಸ್ಯಾಮಃ ।

    ಅನುದ್ಧತತ್ವ-ಪ್ರದರ್ಶನ-ಅರ್ಥಃ ಯದಿ-ಶಬ್ದಃ ನ ಅಜ್ಞಾನ-ಸಂಶಯ-ಅರ್ಥಃ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ-ನಿರ್ಣಯಾತ್ ಅವಸೀಯತೇ ।

    ಸರ್ವಂ ಹ ವಃ ಪೃಷ್ಟ-ಅರ್ಥಂ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮಃ ಇತಿ ॥

    Overall Translation

    To those who had approached thus, the sage (Pippalāda) indeed said — again, once more: "Even though you were already ascetics before, nevertheless here (with me) — through austerity, which is conquest of the senses.

    More specifically — through brahmacarya and through faith which is the disposition of one who believes (āstikyabuddhi) — being devoted and respectful, you shall dwell here for a year, being fully devoted to serving the teacher well.

    Thereafter, ask questions as you wish — each according to his desire, without transgressing it; whatever each one has a desire to know, ask questions on those topics.

    If we shall know what you have asked —

    The word "yadi" (if) is for the purpose of showing humility (anuddhatva), not for expressing ignorance or doubt — this is understood from the fact that all the questions are resolved (answered). We shall tell you all — everything concerning what you have asked."

    Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

    Two passages require explicit anvaya — the complex participial structure of the dwelling-instruction, and the logic of the "yadi" (if).

    Sentence 1 — The condition for dwelling and asking

    As written:

    ಯದ್ಯಪಿ ಯೂಯಂ ಪೂರ್ವಂ ತಪಸ್ವಿನಃ ಏವ, ತಥಾಪಿ ಇಹ ತಪಸಾ ಇಂದ್ರಿಯಸಂಯಮೇನ ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ ಚ ಆಸ್ತಿಕ್ಯಬುದ್ಧ್ಯಾ ಆದರವಂತಃ ಸಂವತ್ಸರಂ ಕಾಲಂ ಸಂವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ ಸಮ್ಯಕ್-ಗುರು-ಶುಶ್ರೂಷಾ-ಪರಾಃ ಸಂತಃ ವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ ।

    Anvaya (resolved prose order):

    ಯದ್ಯಪಿ ಯೂಯಂ ಪೂರ್ವಂ ತಪಸ್ವಿನಃ ಏವ [ಆಸ್ಥ] — ತಥಾಪಿ ಇಹ [ಮದ್ಗೃಹೇ] — [೧] ತಪಸಾ [= ಇಂದ್ರಿಯಸಂಯಮೇನ], [೨] ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ, [೩] ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ [= ಆಸ್ತಿಕ್ಯಬುದ್ಧ್ಯಾ] — ಆದರವಂತಃ [ಸಂತಃ], ಸಮ್ಯಕ್-ಗುರು-ಶುಶ್ರೂಷಾ-ಪರಾಃ ಸಂತಃ — ಸಂವತ್ಸರಂ ಕಾಲಂ ವತ್ಸ್ಯಥ ।

    English: Even though you were previously already ascetics — nevertheless here (in my household), being respectful and fully devoted to serving the teacher well — through (1) austerity (which is conquest of the senses), (2) more specifically through brahmacarya, and (3) through faith (which is the disposition of belief) — you shall dwell for the period of one year.

    Note: Ānandagiri flags that "tathāpi" (even so) connects back to "tapasā" (with austerity), not just to brahmacarya and śraddhā. And "viśeṣataḥ" (more specifically) connects backwards as well — it qualifies the entire preceding phrase about tapas and also what follows. This double connection is the anvaya difficulty Ānandagiri identifies.

    Sentence 2 — The meaning of "yadi" (if)

    As written:

    ಯದಿ ತತ್ ಯುಷ್ಮತ್ ಪೃಷ್ಟಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾಸ್ಯಾಮಃ — ಅನುದ್ಧತತ್ವ-ಪ್ರದರ್ಶನ-ಅರ್ಥಃ ಯದಿ-ಶಬ್ದಃ ನ ಅಜ್ಞಾನ-ಸಂಶಯ-ಅರ್ಥಃ ।

    Anvaya (resolved prose order):

    "ಯದಿ ವಿಜ್ಞಾಸ್ಯಾಮಃ" ಇತಿ ಯದಿ-ಶಬ್ದಃ — [ಅರ್ಥಃ ಕಃ?] ನ ಅಜ್ಞಾನ-ಅರ್ಥಃ, ನ ಸಂಶಯ-ಅರ್ಥಃ — ಅನುದ್ಧತತ್ವ-ಪ್ರದರ್ಶನ-ಅರ್ಥಃ [ಏವ] ।

    [ಕುತಃ ಜ್ಞಾಯತೇ?] ಪ್ರಶ್ನ-ನಿರ್ಣಯಾತ್ [= ಸರ್ವೇ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಾಃ ನಿರ್ಣೀತಾಃ ಇತಿ ದ್ರಷ್ಟ್ವಾ] ।

    English: The word "yadi" (if) in "if we shall know" — what is its meaning? It does not express ignorance, nor does it express doubt — it is solely for the purpose of showing humility (anuddhatva). How is this known? From the fact that all the questions are indeed resolved and answered (in the text that follows).

    Note: This is a hermeneutical observation about the teacher's language. A Ṛṣi of Pippalāda's stature cannot be expressing genuine uncertainty. The "yadi" is a rhetorical humility marker — a conventional expression of non-arrogance in the teacher-student relationship. Śaṅkara makes this explicit to prevent the wrong inference that Pippalāda might not know the answers.

    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

    ತಥಾಪಿ ಇತಿ ಅಸ್ಯ ತಪಸಾ ಇತಿ ಅತಃ ಪೂರ್ವಂ ಅನ್ವಯಃ ।

    ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ಇತಿ ಅಸ್ಯ ಪೂರ್ವತ್ρ ಅಪಿ ಅನ್ವಯಃ ।

    ನಿಷ್ಕೃಷ್ಟಂ ಅರ್ಥಂ ಆಹ – ಯದ್ವಿಷಯ ಇತಿ ।

    ಅಜ್ಞಾನ-ಆದಿ-ಅರ್ಥತ್ವ-ಅಭಾವೇ ಹೇತುಂ ಆಹ – ಪ್ರಶ್ನ ಇತಿ ।

    ಅತ್ρ ಇತಿಶಬ್ದಃ ಅಧ್ಯಾಹಾರ್ಯಃ ।

    ಸರ್ವ-ಪ್ರಶ್ನಾನಾಂ ನಿರ್ಣಯಾತ್ ಅಜ್ಞಾನ-ಆದಿ-ಅಸಂಭವಾತ್ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ॥ ೨ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Ānandagiri gives two syntactic observations on the Bhāṣya's structure:

    First — the word "tathāpi" (even so) in the Bhāṣya connects not only forward to "brahmacarya" etc. but backward — its anvaya is to "tapasā" (with austerity), which comes before it. The sentence should be read: "even so — through austerity (= conquest of the senses)…" The "even so" qualifies the entire condition of dwelling, beginning with tapas.

    Second — the word "viśeṣataḥ" (more specifically) also connects backward — it qualifies what precedes it (tapas) as well as what follows (brahmacarya). So the meaning is: "more specifically — (both tapas and) brahmacarya."

    Ānandagiri then notes that Śaṅkara gives the distilled (niṣkṛṣṭa) meaning of "yathākāmam" — "as you wish" — with the words "yadvishaya" etc. Each student asks about whichever topic he personally has a desire to know; that is the real meaning of "yathākāmam pṛcchata."

    On the reason why "yadi" does not express ignorance: Ānandagiri states the reason — with the word "praśna" (question/resolution). Here the word "iti" is to be supplied (adhyāhārya) — i.e., "from the fact that all the questions are resolved." The meaning is: since all the questions are indeed given their determinations (nirnaya) in the text, the possibility of ignorance or doubt on Pippalāda's part is ruled out.

    ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ಇತಿ ಅಸ್ಯ — of the word "viśeṣataḥ (more specifically)" · ಪೂರ್ವತ್ρ ಅಪಿ — also backward / to what precedes · ಅನ್ವಯಃ — is the syntactic connection ।

    ನಿಷ್ಕೃಷ್ಟಂ ಅರ್ಥಂ — the distilled / extracted meaning · ಆಹ — states · ಯದ್ವಿಷಯ ಇತಿ — with the words "yadvishaya (on whatever topic)" ।

    Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

    Text Author Function
    ಮೂಲ (Mūla) Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) Pippalāda prescribes one year of tapas, brahmacarya, and faith before the students may ask their questions; uses the humble "yadi" (if)
    ಭಾṣya (Bhāṣya) Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) Defines tapas as conquest of the senses; brahmacarya and śraddhā as more specific disciplines; defines "yathākāmam" as each student asking about his own topic; explains "yadi" as humility, not ignorance
    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā) Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) Provides two anvaya corrections — "tathāpi" connects to "tapasā" before it; "viśeṣataḥ" connects backward as well; gives the reason "yadi" cannot mean ignorance — because all questions are resolved in the text

    Core Philosophical Thread

    1. The one-year discipline is not redundant — even though the students are already ascetics, the specific discipline of living with the teacher (gurukula) is different from their prior practice. Tapas here means sense-restraint in the teacher's presence; brahmacarya means disciplined conduct; śraddhā means the disposition of belief (āstikyabuddhi) — trusting the Śruti and the teacher.
    2. "Yathākāmam" is not arbitrary — Śaṅkara's gloss makes this precise: each student asks about whichever topic he personally desires to know, without transgressing that desire (i.e., without asking about something other than what he genuinely wants to know). This connects to the six Praśnas that follow — each student's question reflects his individual jijñāsā.
    3. "Yadi" as anuddhatva — the teacher's use of "if" is a humility convention, not an expression of doubt. Ānandagiri's proof is elegant: since Pippalāda answers all six questions completely, the "if" cannot mean ignorance.
    4. Adhyāhāra in the Ṭīkā — Ānandagiri notes that "iti" must be supplied (adhyāhārya) to complete the sentence "from the fact that all the questions are resolved." This is a minor but characteristic example of the Ṭīkā's grammatical precision.
    5. The double anvaya of "viśeṣataḥ" is Ānandagiri's most subtle grammatical observation in this verse — the word qualifies both what precedes (tapas) and what follows (brahmacarya), giving: "more specifically — (both tapas and) brahmacarya." This prevents reading brahmacarya as simply one item in a list separate from tapas.
