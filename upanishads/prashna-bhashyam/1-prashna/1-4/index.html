    ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೪

    Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 4

    Source of Ṭīkā: Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

    ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

    ತಸ್ಮೈ ಸ ಹೋವಾಚ ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮೋ ವೈ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಸ ತಪೋಽತಪ್ಯತ ಸ ತಪಸ್ತಪ್ತ್ವಾ ಸ ಮಿಥುನಮುತ್ಪಾದಯತೇ ರಯಿಂ ಚ ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಚ ।

    ಇತ್ಯೇತೌ ಮೇ ಬಹುಧಾ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಕರಿಷ್ಯತ ಇತಿ ॥ ೪ ॥

    Overall Translation

    To him (Katyāyana) the sage replied: Prajāpati, desirous of progeny, performed austerity. Having performed austerity, he produced a pair — Rayi and Prāṇa — (thinking): "These two will produce creatures for me in many ways."

    Word-to-Word

    ತಸ್ಮೈ — to him (Katyāyana) · ಸಃ ಹ ಉವಾಚ — he (Pippalāda) indeed said · ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮಃ — desirous of progeny · ವೈ — indeed · ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ — Prajāpati (Lord of creatures) · ಸಃ — he · ತಪಃ ಅತಪ್ಯತ — performed austerity · ಸಃ — he · ತಪಃ ತಪ್ತ್ವಾ — having performed austerity · ಸಃ — he · ಮಿಥುನಂ — a pair / dyad · ಉತ್ಪಾದಯತೇ — produced ।

    ರಯಿಂ ಚ — Rayi (the material principle) and · ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಚ — Prāṇa (the life-force) and · ಇತಿ — (thinking) thus · ಏತೌ — these two · ಮೇ — for me · ಬಹುಧಾ — in many ways · ಪ್ರಜಾಃ — creatures / progeny · ಕರಿಷ್ಯತಃ — will produce · ಇತಿ — thus ॥

    ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

    ತಸ್ಮೈ ಏವಂ ಪೃಷ್ಟವತೇ ಸ ಹ ಉವಾಚ ತದಪಾಕರಣಾಯ ಆಹ — ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಆತ್ಮನಃ ಸಿಸೃಕ್ಷುಃ ವೈ ।

    ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ಸನ್ ಜಗತ್ ಸ್ರಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮಿ ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾನವಾನ್ ಯಥೋಕ್ತಕಾರೀ ತದ್ಭಾವಭಾವಿತಃ ಕಲ್ಪಾದೌ ನಿರ್ವೃತ್ತಃ ಹಿರಣ್ಯಗರ್ಭಃ, ಸೃಜ್ಯಮಾನಾನಾಂ ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ ಸ್ಥಾವರಜಂಗಮಾನಾಂ ಪತಿಃ ಸನ್ ।

    ಜನ್ಮಾಂತರಭಾವಿತಂ ಜ್ಞಾನಂ ಶ್ರುತಿಪ್ರಕಾಶಿತಾರ್ಥವಿಷಯಂ ತಪಃ, ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚಯತ್ ಅತಪ್ಯತ ।

    ಅಥ ತು ಸಃ ಏವಂ ತಪಃ ತಪ್ತ್ವಾ ಶ್ರೌತಂ ಜ್ಞಾನಂ ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚ್ಯ, ಸೃಷ್ಟಿಸಾಧನಭೂತಂ ಮಿಥುನಂ ಉತ್ಪಾದಯತೇ ಮಿಥುನಂ ದ್ವಂದ್ವಂ ಉತ್ಪಾದಿತವಾನ್ ರಯಿಂ ಚ ಸೋಮಂ ಅನ್ನಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಚ ಅಗ್ನಿಂ ಅತ್ತಾರಮ್ ।

    ಇತ್ಯೇತೌ ಅಗ್ನೀಷೋಮೌ ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನಭೂತೌ ಮೇ ಮಮ ಬಹುಧಾ ಅನೇಕಧಾ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಕರಿಷ್ಯತಃ ಇತಿ ಏವಂ ಸಂಚಿಂತ್ಯ ಅಂಡೋತ್ಪತ್ತಿಕ್ರಮೇಣ ಸೂರ್ಯಾ-ಚಂದ್ರಮಸೌ ಅಕಲ್ಪಯತ್ ॥

    Overall Translation

    To the one who had asked thus, he (Pippalāda) replied — for the purpose of removing (answering) that (question): Prajāpati was indeed desirous of progeny — desirous of creating creatures from himself.

    Prajāpati — being the Self of all, knowing "I shall create the world," performing what was enjoined (jñāna and karma combined), saturated with that very contemplation (of being Prajāpati), arising at the beginning of the kalpa as Hiraṇyagarbha, and being the lord of the creatures — both moving and stationary — that are to be created:

    He deliberated upon / performed austerity — the knowledge cultivated through many births, concerned with the meanings revealed by the Śruti — he contemplated, he practised.

    Then, having thus performed that austerity — having deliberated upon Vedic knowledge — he produced the pair (mithuna) that is the instrument of creation; he produced the dyad: Rayi — the Soma, the food — and Prāṇa — the Fire, the eater.

    Thinking thus — "these two, Agni and Soma, which are the eater and the food, will produce creatures for me in many ways" — he fashioned Sun and Moon in the sequence of the creation of the cosmic egg (aṇḍa).

    Word-to-Word

    ತಸ್ಮೈ — to that one · ಏವಂ ಪೃಷ್ಟವತೇ — who had asked thus · ಸ ಹ ಉವಾಚ — he indeed said · ತದಪಾಕರಣಾಯ — for the purpose of removing / answering that · ಆಹ — he stated · ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮಃ — desirous of progeny · ಪ್ರಜಾಃ — creatures · ಆತ್ಮನಃ — from himself · ಸಿಸೃಕ್ಷುಃ — desirous of creating · ವೈ — indeed ।

    ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ — Prajāpati · ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ಸನ್ — being the Self of all · ಜಗತ್ ಸ್ರಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮಿ — I shall create the world · ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ — thinking thus · ವಿಜ್ಞಾನವಾನ್ — possessed of knowledge · ಯಥೋಕ್ತಕಾರೀ — performing what was enjoined · ತದ್ಭಾವಭಾವಿತಃ — saturated with that contemplation · ಕಲ್ಪಾದೌ — at the beginning of the kalpa · ನಿರ್ವೃತ್ತಃ — arisen / manifested · ಹಿರಣ್ಯಗರ್ಭಃ — Hiraṇyagarbha · ಸೃಜ್ಯಮಾನಾನಾಂ — of those to be created · ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ — of creatures · ಸ್ಥಾವರಜಂಗಮಾನಾಂ — both stationary and moving · ಪತಿಃ ಸನ್ — being the lord ।

    ಜನ್ಮಾಂತರಭಾವಿತಂ — cultivated through many births · ಜ್ಞಾನಂ — knowledge · ಶ್ರುತಿಪ್ರಕಾಶಿತ-ಅರ್ಥ-ವಿಷಯಂ — concerned with meanings revealed by the Śruti · ತಪಃ — austerity · ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚಯತ್ — deliberated upon · ಅತಪ್ಯತ — performed austerity ।

    ಅಥ ತು — then however · ಸಃ — he · ಏವಂ ತಪಃ ತಪ್ತ್ವಾ — having thus performed austerity · ಶ್ರೌತಂ ಜ್ಞಾನಂ — Vedic knowledge · ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚ್ಯ — having deliberated upon · ಸೃಷ್ಟಿಸಾಧನಭೂತಂ — that which is the instrument of creation · ಮಿಥುನಂ — the pair · ಉತ್ಪಾದಯತೇ — produced · ದ್ವಂದ್ವಂ — the dyad · ಉತ್ಪಾದಿತವಾನ್ — he produced · ರಯಿಂ ಚ — Rayi and · ಸೋಮಂ — Soma · ಅನ್ನಂ — food · ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಚ — and Prāṇa · ಅಗ್ನಿಂ — Fire · ಅತ್ತಾರಂ — the eater ।

    ಇತ್ಯೇತೌ — these two · ಅಗ್ನೀಷೋಮೌ — Agni and Soma · ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನಭೂತೌ — being the eater and the food · ಮೇ ಮಮ — for me · ಬಹುಧಾ ಅನೇಕಧಾ — in many / manifold ways · ಪ್ರಜಾಃ — creatures · ಕರಿಷ್ಯತಃ — will produce · ಇತಿ ಏವಂ ಸಂಚಿಂತ್ಯ — thinking thus · ಅಂಡೋತ್ಪತ್ತಿ-ಕ್ರಮೇಣ — in the sequence of creation of the cosmic egg · ಸೂರ್ಯ-ಚಂದ್ರಮಸೌ — Sun and Moon · ಅಕಲ್ಪಯತ್ — he fashioned / created ॥

    Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

    The Bhāṣya's main sentence describing Prajāpati is the most complex in this verse — a long nominative chain with multiple participials before the finite verb.

    Sentence — Prajāpati as Hiraṇyagarbha performing austerity

    As written:

    ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ಸನ್ ಜಗತ್ ಸ್ರಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮಿ ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾನವಾನ್ ಯಥೋಕ್ತಕಾರೀ ತದ್ಭಾವಭಾವಿತಃ ಕಲ್ಪಾದೌ ನಿರ್ವೃತ್ತಃ ಹಿರಣ್ಯಗರ್ಭಃ ಸ್ಥಾವರಜಂಗಮಾನಾಂ ಪತಿಃ ಸನ್ ಜನ್ಮಾಂತರಭಾವಿತಂ ಜ್ಞಾನಂ ತಪಃ ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚಯತ್ ಅತಪ್ಯತ ।

    Anvaya (resolved prose order):

    [ಸಃ] ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ — [೧] ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ಸನ್, [೨] "ಜಗತ್ ಸ್ರಕ್ಷ್ಯಾಮಿ" ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾನವಾನ್, [೩] ಯಥೋಕ್ತಕಾರೀ [ಜ್ಞಾನಕರ್ಮಸಮುಚ್ಚಯಕಾರೀ], [೪] ತದ್ಭಾವಭಾವಿತಃ [ಉಪಾಸನಾಕಾಲೀನ-ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿ-ಭಾವನಾ-ಯುಕ್ತಃ], [೫] ಕಲ್ಪಾದೌ ಹಿರಣ್ಯಗರ್ಭಾತ್ಮನಾ ನಿರ್ವೃತ್ತಃ, [೬] ಸ್ಥಾವರಜಂಗಮ-ಪ್ರಜಾ-ಪತಿಃ ಸನ್ — ಜನ್ಮಾಂತರಭಾವಿತಂ ಶ್ರೌತ-ಜ್ಞಾನ-ವಿಷಯಂ ತಪಃ ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚಯತ್ [ಅಥ] ಅತಪ್ಯತ ।

    English: That Prajāpati — [1] being the Self of all, [2] knowing "I shall create the world," [3] performing what was enjoined (combining knowledge and action), [4] saturated with the contemplation of being Prajāpati (during his prior meditation), [5] arisen at the beginning of the kalpa as Hiraṇyagarbha, [6] being the lord of moving and stationary creatures — deliberated upon and performed austerity, which is knowledge cultivated through many births and directed at the meanings revealed by the Śruti.

    Note: Six participial/adjectival descriptions accumulate before the two finite verbs (ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚಯತ್ and ಅತಪ್ಯತ). Ānandagiri unpacks each one with a specific technical term — particularly "yathoktakārī" (performing what was enjoined) as jñāna-karma-samuccaya-kārī, and "tadbhāvabhāvitaḥ" as upāsanākālīna-prajāpati- bhāvanā-yuktaḥ.

    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

    ತಸ್ಮೈ ಸ ಹೋವಾಚ ಇತಿ ಪ್ರತಿಜ್ಞಾತಂ ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ದರ್ಶಯತಿ – ತದಪಾಕರಣಾಯ ಇತಿ ।

    ಆದ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಸನ್ ಇತ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮಃ ಸನ್ ಇತಿ ಅನ್ವಯಃ ।

    ಯಥೋಕ್ತಕಾರೀ ಇತಿ ।

    ಜ್ಞಾನಕರ್ಮಸಮುಚ್ಚಯಕಾರೀ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ತದ್ಭಾವಭಾವಿತ ಇತಿ ।

    ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿರಹಂ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ಇತಿ ಉಪಾಸನಕಾಲೀನ-ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿ-ಭಾವನಾ-ಯುಕ್ತಃ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ಪೂರ್ವಕಲ್ಪೀಯ-ತದ್ಭಾವ-ಭಾವಿತಃ ಏತತ್ಕಲ್ಪಾದೌ ಹಿರಣ್ಯಗರ್ಭಾತ್ಮನಾ ನಿರ್ವೃತ್ತಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಸನ್ ಪಶ್ಚಾತ್ ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮಃ ಸನ್ ತಪಃ ಜನ್ಮಾಂತರಭಾವಿತಂ ಜ್ಞಾನಂ ಶ್ರುತಿಪ್ರಕಾಶಿತ-ಅರ್ಥ-ವಿಷಯಂ ಅತಪ್ಯತ ಅನ್ವಾಲೋಚಯತ್ ಚಿಂತಾದಿನಾ ತತ್ಸಂಸ್ಕಾರಂ ಉದ್ಬೋಧ್ಯ ಜ್ಞಾನಂ ಉತ್ಪಾದಿತವಾನ್ ಇತಿ ಅನ್ವಯಃ ।

    ತತ್ρ ಪ್ರಥಮಂ ಆದಿತ್ಯಚಂದ್ರ-ಉತ್ಪಾದನೇನ ತದ್ಭಾವಂ ಆಪದ್ಯ ಪಶ್ಚಾತ್ ಚಂದ್ರಾದಿತ್ಯ-ಸಾಧ್ಯ-ಸಂವತ್ಸರ-ಭಾವಂ ಆಪದ್ಯ ಏವಮೇವ ತದವಯವ-ಅಯನದ್ವಯ-ಮಾಸ-ಪಕ್ಷ-ಅಹೋರಾತ್ರ-ಭಾವಂ ಆಪದ್ಯ ತತಃ ತತ್ಸಾಧ್ಯ-ವ್ರೀಹ್ಯಾದ್ಯನ್ನ-ಭಾವಂ ರೇತಃ-ಭಾವಂ ಚ ಆಪದ್ಯ ತೇನ ರೇತಸಾ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಸೃಜೇಯಂ ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ನಿಶ್ಚಿತ್ಯ ಪ್ರಥಮಂ ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣ-ಶಬ್ದಿತ-ಚಂದ್ರ-ಸೂರ್ಯ-ದ್ವಂದ್ವಂ ಉತ್ಪಾದಿತವಾನ್ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ಸ ಏವಂ ಇತಿ ।

    ರಯಿಶಬ್ದೇನ ಧನವಾಚಿನಾ ಭೋಜ್ಯಜಾತಂ ಲಕ್ಷಯಿತ್ವಾ ಭೋಜ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಸೋಮಕಿರಣ-ಅಮೃತ-ಯುಕ್ತತ್ವಾತ್ ತದ್ದ್ವಾರಾ ಸೋಮಃ ಲಕ್ಷ್ಯತೇ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ರಯಿಂ ಚ ಇತಿ ।

    ಏವಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಶಬ್ದೇನ ಅಪಿ ।

    "ಅಹಂ ವೈಶ್ವಾನರೋ ಭೂತ್ವಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಿನಾಂ ದೇಹಮಾಶ್ರಿತಃ । ಪ್ರಾಣಾಪಾನಸಮಾಯುಕ್ತಃ ಪಚಾಮ್ಯನ್ನಂ ಚತುರ್ವಿಧಮ್ ॥" [ಭಗವದ್ಗೀತಾ ೧೫।೧೪] ಇತಿ ಸ್ಮೃತೇಃ ಅಗ್ನೇಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಸಂಬಂಧಾತ್ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಭೋಕ್ತಾ ಲಕ್ಷ್ಯತೇ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಚ ಇತಿ ।

    ಅಗ್ನೀಷೋಮಯೋಃ ಅಂಡ-ಅಂತರ್ಗತತ್ವೇನ ಅಂಡೋತ್ಪತ್ತಿ-ಅನಂತರಂ ಉತ್ಪತ್ತಿಃ ಇತ್ಯಾಶಯೇನ ಆಹ – ಅಂಡೋತ್ಪತ್ತಿ ಇತಿ ।

    "ಉದ್ಯಂತಂ ವಾವ ಆದಿತ್ಯಂ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಅನುಸಮಾರೋಹತಿ" ಇತಿ ಶ್ರುತೇಃ ಸೂರ್ಯ-ಅಗ್ನ್ಯೋಃ ಏಕತ್ವಂ ಅಭಿಪ್ರೇತ್ಯ ಅಗ್ನಿಂ ಸೂರ್ಯಪದೇನ ಆಹ – ಸೂರ್ಯ-ಚಂದ್ರಮಸೌ ಇತಿ ॥ ೪ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Ānandagiri opens by noting that what was announced in principle (pratijñāta) in the first verse of the Praśna — Pippalāda will answer the question — is now being shown in detail by Śaṅkara — with the words "tadapākaraṇāya" (for the purpose of removing / answering that question).

    On the first occurrence of "san" (being) in the Bhāṣya: Ānandagiri gives the anvaya — it connects to "prajākāmaḥ san" (being desirous of progeny). This clarifies the syntactic flow of Śaṅkara's long sentence.

    On "yathoktakārī" (performing what was enjoined): Ānandagiri clarifies this means "performing the combination of knowledge and action" (jñāna-karma-samuccaya-kārī) — i.e., Hiraṇyagarbha is not a mere ritualist nor a mere meditator, but one who combines both.

    On "tadbhāvabhāvitaḥ" (saturated with that contemplation): Ānandagiri explains this means "endowed with the meditation on being Prajāpati who is the Self of all — practised during the time of upāsanā (meditation)." Then Ānandagiri provides the full anvaya of this dense passage: Prajāpati — saturated with that Prajāpati- contemplation from the previous kalpa — having arisen at the beginning of this kalpa as Hiraṇyagarbha, and then being desirous of progeny — performed austerity (which is the knowledge cultivated through many births, directed at Śruti-meanings) — by contemplation and reflection, he awakened those saṃskāras (latent impressions) and produced knowledge.

    Ānandagiri then provides the complete sequential logic of Prajāpati's plan: first by producing Sun and Moon, then by taking on the nature of the Year (produced through Moon and Sun), then by taking on the natures of the two ayanas, Month, Fortnight, and Day-and-Night (the components of the Year), then by taking on the nature of food (rice etc.) produced through those, and then seed — deciding "I shall create creatures through that seed" — and having resolved this plan, he first produced the Moon-Sun dyad designated as Rayi and Prāṇa. This is stated with the words "sa evam" (he thus).

    On "rayiṃ ca" (and Rayi): Ānandagiri explains that the word "rayi" (which means wealth) indicates the class of what is to be enjoyed (bhokhya-jāta); and since what is to be enjoyed is associated with Soma's rays and nectar — through that, Soma (the Moon) is indicated. Similarly with the word "prāṇa."

    Citing the Bhagavad Gītā (15.14): "Having become Vaiśvānara, residing in the bodies of living beings, combined with prāṇa and apāna, I digest the fourfold food" — because Fire is connected to Prāṇa through this Smṛti, Fire is indicated as the enjoyer (bhoktā) — with the words "prāṇaṃ ca."

    On "aṇḍotpatti" (creation of the cosmic egg): Ānandagiri explains that since Agni and Soma are contained within the cosmic egg, their arising comes after the creation of the egg — this is the intent.

    On "sūryacandramasau" (Sun and Moon): Ānandagiri notes that based on the Śruti — "Fire follows the rising Sun" — the identity of Sun and Fire is intended, and therefore Fire is referred to by the word "Sun." This is stated with the words "sūryacandramasau."

    Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

    Text Author Function
    ಮೂಲ (Mūla) Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) States that Prajāpati performed austerity and produced the pair — Rayi and Prāṇa — to generate creatures
    ಭಾṣya (Bhāṣya) Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) Identifies Prajāpati with Hiraṇyagarbha; defines tapas as Śruti-directed knowledge cultivated over many births; identifies Rayi = Soma = food and Prāṇa = Fire = eater; describes the sequential plan of creation from Sun-Moon to seed
    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā) Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) Provides the anvaya of the dense Bhāṣya sentence; clarifies yathoktakārī as jñāna-karma-samuccaya; unpacks tadbhāvabhāvita as upāsanā-meditation; gives the full sequential cosmogonic plan; explains rayi and prāṇa as upalakaṣaṇas for Soma and Agni respectively; cites BG 15.14 for Fire-Prāṇa identity; cites Śruti for Sun-Fire identity

    Core Philosophical Thread

    1. This verse is the cosmogonic answer to the first Praśna's question — from where are creatures born? Prajāpati creates the Rayi-Prāṇa pair as the first step in a chain that ends with seed and creatures.
    2. Prajāpati = Hiraṇyagarbha — the first-born, the cosmic egg, arising at the beginning of each kalpa. His "austerity" is not physical discipline but the contemplation and reawakening of Śruti-knowledge from previous births.
    3. Yathoktakārī = jñāna-karma-samuccaya — Ānandagiri's gloss confirms that Hiraṇyagarbha is the paradigm of the combined path: both knowledge and action, not one alone.
    4. The sequential cosmogonic plan laid out by Ānandagiri in the Ṭīkā is the clearest single summary of the entire first Praśna's argument: Sun-Moon → Year → ayanas → Month → Fortnight → Day-and-Night → food → seed → creatures.
    5. Rayi and Prāṇa as upalakaṣaṇas — the word "rayi" (wealth) indicates the class of what is enjoyed and thereby points to Soma; the word "prāṇa" through its connection to Fire (BG 15.14) points to Agni. This is Ānandagiri's linguistic precision — the Upaniṣad uses these two terms as indicative labels for the Moon-Sun pair.
