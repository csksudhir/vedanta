    ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ದ್ವಿತೀಯ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೬

    Praśna Upaniṣad — Second Question, Verse 6

    Source of Ṭīkā: Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

    ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

    ಅರಾ ಇವ ರಥನಾಭೌ ಪ್ರಾಣೇ ಸರ್ವಂ ಪ್ರತಿಷ್ಠಿತಮ್ ।

    ಋಚೋ ಯಜೂꣳಷಿ ಸಾಮಾನಿ ಯಜ್ಞಃ ಕ್ಷತ್ತ್ರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ ॥ ೬ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Like spokes in the hub of a wheel, all is established in Prāṇa.

    The Ṛk verses, the Yajus formulas, the Sāman chants, sacrifice, the kṣatriya (ruling power), and brahman (the brāhmaṇa order) — all this too.

    Word-to-Word

    ಅರಾ ಇವ — like spokes · ರಥನಾಭೌ — in the hub of a chariot-wheel · ಪ್ರಾಣೇ — in Prāṇa · ಸರ್ವಂ — everything · ಪ್ರತಿಷ್ಠಿತಂ — is established / fixed ।

    ಋಚಃ — the Ṛk verses · ಯಜೂꣳಷಿ — the Yajus formulas · ಸಾಮಾನಿ — the Sāman chants · ಯಜ್ಞಃ — sacrifice · ಕ್ಷತ್ತ್ರಂ — the kṣatriya / ruling power · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ — and brahman (the brāhmaṇa order) ॥

    ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

    ಕಿಂ ಬಹುನಾ ? ಅರಾ ಇವ ರಥನಾಭೌ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಾದಿ-ನಾಮ-ಅಂತಂ ಸರ್ವಂ ಸ್ಥಿತಿಕಾಲೇ ಪ್ರಾಣೇ ಏವ ಪ್ರತಿಷ್ಠಿತಂ ।

    ತಥಾ ಋಚಃ ಯಜೂꣳಷಿ ಸಾಮಾನಿ ಇತಿ ತ್ರಿವಿಧಾ ಮಂತ್ರಾಃ ತತ್ಸಾಧ್ಯಃ ಚ ಯಜ್ಞಃ ಕ್ಷತ್ತ್ರಂ ಚ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಪಾಲಯಿತೃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ ಯಜ್ಞಾದಿ-ಕರ್ಮ-ಕರ್ತೃತ್ವೇ ಅಧಿಕೃತಂ ಚ ಏಷ ಏವ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಸರ್ವಮ್ ॥

    Overall Translation

    Why say more? Like spokes in the hub of a chariot-wheel, all that exists — from śraddhā (faith) at the beginning to nāma (name) at the end — is established in Prāṇa alone at the time of (its) subsistence.

    So too — the Ṛk verses, Yajus formulas, and Sāman chants — the three kinds of mantras — and the sacrifice accomplished through them; and the kṣatriya — the protector of all; and brahman — that which is qualified for the performance of sacrifice and other ritual action — all this is this very Prāṇa.

    Word-to-Word

    ಕಿಂ ಬಹುನಾ — why say more? · ಅರಾ ಇವ — like spokes · ರಥನಾಭೌ — in the hub of a chariot-wheel · ಶ್ರದ್ಧಾದಿ-ನಾಮ-ಅಂತಂ — from śraddhā (faith) at the beginning to nāma (name) at the end · ಸರ್ವಂ — everything · ಸ್ಥಿತಿಕಾಲೇ — at the time of subsistence · ಪ್ರಾಣೇ ಏವ — in Prāṇa alone · ಪ್ರತಿಷ್ಠಿತಂ — is established ।

    ತಥಾ — so too · ಋಚಃ ಯಜೂꣳಷಿ ಸಾಮಾನಿ ಇತಿ — the Ṛk, Yajus, and Sāman · ತ್ರಿವಿಧಾ ಮಂತ್ರಾಃ — the three kinds of mantras · ತತ್ಸಾಧ್ಯಃ ಚ — and what is accomplished through them · ಯಜ್ಞಃ — sacrifice · ಕ್ಷತ್ತ್ರಂ ಚ — and the kṣatriya · ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಪಾಲಯಿತೃ — protector of all · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ — and brahman · ಯಜ್ಞಾದಿ-ಕರ್ಮ-ಕರ್ತೃತ್ವೇ — for the performance of sacrifice and other ritual action · ಅಧಿಕೃತಂ — that which is qualified · ಏಷ ಏವ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — this very Prāṇa · ಸರ್ವಂ — is all this ॥

    Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

    The final sentence accumulates multiple subjects all predicated on the one Prāṇa and requires explicit resolution.

    Sentence — All Vedic and social categories as Prāṇa

    As written:

    ತಥಾ ಋಚಃ ಯಜೂꣳಷಿ ಸಾಮಾನಿ ತ್ರಿವಿಧಾ ಮಂತ್ರಾಃ ತತ್ಸಾಧ್ಯಃ ಚ ಯಜ್ಞಃ ಕ್ಷತ್ತ್ರಂ ಚ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಪಾಲಯಿತೃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ ಯಜ್ಞಾದಿ-ಕರ್ಮ-ಕರ್ತೃತ್ವೇ ಅಧಿಕೃತಂ ಚ — ಏಷ ಏವ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಸರ್ವಂ ।

    Anvaya (resolved prose order):

    ತಥಾ [ಇದಂ ಸರ್ವಂ ಅಪಿ ಏಷ ಏವ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ] — [ಪ್ರಥಮಂ:] ತ್ರಿವಿಧಾ ಮಂತ್ರಾಃ — ಋಚಃ, ಯಜೂꣳಷಿ, ಸಾಮಾನಿ ; [ದ್ವಿತೀಯಂ:] ತತ್ಸಾಧ್ಯಃ ಯಜ್ಞಃ ; [ತೃತೀಯಂ:] ಕ್ಷತ್ತ್ರಂ [= ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಪಾಲಯಿತೃ] ; [ಚತುರ್ಥಂ:] ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ [= ಯಜ್ಞಾದಿ-ಕರ್ಮ-ಕರ್ತೃತ್ವೇ ಅಧಿಕೃತಂ] — [ಏತತ್] ಸರ್ವಂ ಏಷ ಏವ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ।

    English: So too — all this is this very Prāṇa: [1] the three kinds of mantras — Ṛk, Yajus, Sāman; [2] the sacrifice accomplished through them; [3] the kṣatriya (the protector of all); [4] brahman (that which is qualified for the performance of sacrifice and other ritual action) — all this is this very Prāṇa.

    Note: The word "ca" (and) is used three times — after yajña, kṣattra, and brahman. Ānandagiri's gloss on the final "ca" confirms that it draws all three (yajña, kṣattra, brahman) together into the predication "all this is Prāṇa." The anvaya shows four distinct categories all resolved into the one subject.

    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

    ಶ್ರದ್ಧಾದಿ ಇತಿ ।

    ಪ್ರಾಣಾತ್ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಾಂ ಖಂ ವಾಯುಃ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ ಆಪ ಇತಿ ಆದಿನಾ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಮಾಣ-ಷೋಡಶ-ಕಲಾ-ಆತ್ಮಕ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ ಇತಿ ।

    ಬ್ರಾಹ್ಮಣ-ಜಾತಿಃ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।

    ಚ-ಶಬ್ದ-ಅರ್ಥಂ ಆಹ – ಯಜ್ಞಾದಿ ಇತಿ ।

    ಅಧಿಕೃತಂ ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ ಇತಿ ಅನ್ವಯಃ ॥ ೬ ॥

    Overall Translation

    On "śraddhādi" (from śraddhā onwards): Ānandagiri explains that the range "from śraddhā at the beginning to nāma at the end" refers to the sixteen kalas (parts / aspects) of Prāṇa to be stated subsequently — beginning with "from Prāṇa: śraddhā, ākāśa, vāyu, jyotis, water..." etc. (as will be described in the third Praśna). In other words, "all" here means all sixteen aspects of Prāṇa.

    On "brahma ca" (and brahman): Ānandagiri clarifies that "brahma" here means the brāhmaṇa caste / order (brāhmaṇa-jāti) — not the supreme Brahman. It is the social-ritual category of those qualified for sacrifice.

    On the meaning of the word "ca" (and): Ānandagiri explains — with the words "yajñādi" (sacrifice and the like). The syntactic connection (anvaya) is: "that which is qualified — (all this, from) sacrifice onwards — is everything (Prāṇa)." The "ca" draws together the entire list and predicates it on Prāṇa.

    Word-to-Word

    ಶ್ರದ್ಧಾದಿ ಇತಿ — on the phrase "śraddhādi (from śraddhā onwards)" ।

    ಪ್ರಾಣಾತ್ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಾಂ — "from Prāṇa: śraddhā" · ಖಂ ವಾಯುಃ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ ಆಪ ಇತಿ ಆದಿನಾ — "ākāśa, vāyu, light, water" and so on · ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಮಾಣ — to be stated subsequently (in the third Praśna) · ಷೋಡಶ-ಕಲಾ-ಆತ್ಮಕ — of the nature of the sixteen kalas (aspects) · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

    ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಚ ಇತಿ — on the phrase "brahma ca (and brahman)" ।

    ಬ್ರಾಹ್ಮಣ-ಜಾತಿಃ — the brāhmaṇa caste / order · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

    ಚ-ಶಬ್ದ-ಅರ್ಥಂ ಆಹ — states the meaning of the word "ca (and)" · ಯಜ್ಞಾದಿ ಇತಿ — with the word "yajñādi (sacrifice and the like)" ।

    ಅಧಿಕೃತಂ — that which is qualified · ಸರ್ವಂ ಏವ — is everything (Prāṇa) · ಇತಿ ಅನ್ವಯಃ — thus is the syntactic connection ॥

    Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

    Text Author Function
    ಮೂಲ (Mūla) Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) Continues the praise — using the spoke-hub analogy, everything (Vedic mantras, sacrifice, kṣatriya, and brahman) is established in Prāṇa
    ಭಾṣya (Bhāṣya) Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) Explains "all" as ranging from śraddhā to nāma (the sixteen kalas); explicates kṣattra as the protector of all and brahman as the brāhmaṇa qualified for ritual; closes with "all this is this very Prāṇa"
    ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā) Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) Identifies śraddhādi as pointing to the sixteen kalas of the third Praśna; clarifies brahma = brāhmaṇa-jāti (not supreme Brahman); explains the syntactic role of "ca" as gathering all into the one predication

    Core Philosophical Thread

    1. The spoke-hub analogy — first seen in the first Praśna (verse 11, "arā iva rathānābhau") — is now applied directly to Prāṇa: all things are fixed in Prāṇa as spokes in a hub. The analogy recurs because the second Praśna is establishing Prāṇa's supremacy within the body in parallel to the cosmic analysis of the first Praśna.
    2. Śraddhā to nāma — the "all" of the spoke-hub statement ranges from śraddhā (faith) to nāma (name). Ānandagiri identifies this as the sixteen kalas (aspects/digits) of Prāṇa to be described in the third Praśna — giving the verse a forward-looking structural function.
    3. Three categories of Vedic reality — mantras (Ṛk, Yajus, Sāman), sacrifice (accomplished through them), and the two social orders (kṣatriya and brāhmaṇa) — together represent the entirety of Vedic civilisation. All of it is Prāṇa.
    4. Brahma = brāhmaṇa-jāti — Ānandagiri's clarification prevents confusion with parabrahman. The verse is about the social-ritual category of those qualified for sacrifice, not the supreme reality.
    5. "Kiṃ bahunā?" (why say more?) — the Bhāṣya's opening rhetorical question signals that the catalogue of Prāṇa's identifications could continue indefinitely; the verse makes the general statement to close it.
