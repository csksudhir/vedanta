# Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.4.10

## ಮಂತ್ರ

<div class="mantra">
ಯದೇವೇಹ ತದಮುತ್ರ ಯದಮುತ್ರ ತದನ್ವಿಹ ।
ಮೃತ್ಯೋಃ ಸ ಮೃತ್ಯುಮಾಪ್ನೋತಿ ಯ ಇಹ ನಾನೇವ ಪಶ್ಯತಿ ॥ ೧೦ ॥
</div>

<div class="translation">
Whatever is here, that indeed is there; and whatever is there, that indeed is here. He who sees even the slightest plurality here goes from death to death.
</div>

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ

<div class="bhasya">
ಯದ್ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಾದಿಸ್ಥಾವರಾಂತೇಷು ವರ್ತಮಾನಂ ತತ್ತದುಪಾಧಿತ್ವಾದಬ್ರಹ್ಮವದವಭಾಸಮಾನಂ ಸಂಸಾರ್ಯನ್ಯತ್ಪರಸ್ಮಾದ್ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಣ ಇತಿ ಮಾ ಭೂತ್ಕಸ್ಯಚಿದಾಶಂಕೇತೀದಮಾಹ — ಯದೇವೇಹ ಕಾರ್ಯಕಾರಣೋಪಾಧಿಸಮನ್ವಿತಂ ಸಂಸಾರಧರ್ಮವದವಭಾಸಮಾನಮವಿವೇಕಿನಾಮ್ , ತದೇವ ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಸ್ಥಮ್ ಅಮುತ್ರ ನಿತ್ಯವಿಜ್ಞಾನಘನಸ್ವಭಾವಂ ಸರ್ವಸಂಸಾರಧರ್ಮವರ್ಜಿತಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ । ಯಚ್ಚ ಅಮುತ್ರ ಅಮುಷ್ಮಿನ್ನಾತ್ಮನಿ ಸ್ಥಿತಮ್ , ತದನು ಇಹ ತದೇವ ಇಹ ಕಾರ್ಯಕರಣನಾಮರೂಪೋಪಾಧಿಮ್ ಅನು ವಿಭಾವ್ಯಮಾನಂ ನಾನ್ಯತ್ । ತತ್ರೈವಂ ಸತಿ ಉಪಾಧಿಸ್ವಭಾವಭೇದದೃಷ್ಟಿಲಕ್ಷಣಯಾ ಅವಿದ್ಯಯಾ ಮೋಹಿತಃ ಸನ್ ಯ ಇಹ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಣ್ಯನಾನಾಭೂತೇ ಪರಸ್ಮಾದನ್ಯೋಽಹಂ ಮತ್ತೋಽನ್ಯತ್ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮೇತಿ ನಾನೇವ ಭಿನ್ನಮಿವ ಪಶ್ಯತಿ ಉಪಲಭತೇ, ಸ ಮೃತ್ಯೋರ್ಮರಣಾತ್ ಮೃತ್ಯುಂ ಮರಣಂ ಪುನಃ ಪುನಃ ಜನನಮರಣಭಾವಮಾಪ್ನೋತಿ ಪ್ರತಿಪದ್ಯತೇ । ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ತಥಾ ನ ಪಶ್ಯೇತ್ । ವಿಜ್ಞಾನೈಕರಸಂ ನೈರಂತರ್ಯೇಣಾಕಾಶವತ್ಪರಿಪೂರ್ಣಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮೈವಾಹಮಸ್ಮೀತಿ ಪಶ್ಯೇದಿತಿ ವಾಕ್ಯಾರ್ಥಃ ॥
</div>

<div class="translation">
Lest anyone entertain the doubt that the Self appearing in all beings—from Brahmā down to the smallest stationary object—is something different from the Supreme Brahman because it appears conditioned and subject to saṃsāra, the Upaniṣad declares this mantra.

That which appears here to the undiscriminating as associated with causes, effects, and limiting adjuncts, and therefore as possessing the characteristics of worldly existence, is itself the very Brahman that exists in its own nature, there (in reality), as pure consciousness, eternal, homogeneous knowledge, and free from all attributes of saṃsāra.

And that which exists there in the Self is precisely what appears here through the adjuncts of body, senses, names, and forms. It is not something different.

When this is so, a person deluded by ignorance, characterized by perceiving differences arising from limiting adjuncts, imagines:

"I am different from Brahman; Brahman is something other than me."

Seeing plurality as though it were real, he repeatedly attains death—that is, repeated birth and death.

Therefore one should not see in that manner.

Rather, one should realize:

"I am Brahman alone—of the nature of pure consciousness, continuous, and all-pervading like space."

This is the meaning of the passage.
</div>

### ಭಾಷ್ಯ ಪದಾರ್ಥ
