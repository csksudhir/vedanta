# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೩
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 3

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ಅಥ ಕಬಂಧೀ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನ ಉಪೇತ್ಯ ಪಪ್ರಚ್ಛ ಭಗವನ್ ಕುತೋ ಹ ವಾ ಇಮಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತ ಇತಿ ॥ ೩ ॥

### Overall Translation

Then Kabandhi Kātyāyana approached (Pippalāda) and asked: "Revered Sir, from
where indeed are these creatures born?"

### Word-to-Word

ಅಥ — then / next · ಕಬಂಧೀ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನಃ — Kabandhi Kātyāyana (the student's name and lineage) · ಉಪೇತ್ಯ — having approached / coming near · ಪಪ್ರಚ್ಛ — asked / enquired · ಭಗವನ್ — O Revered Sir / O Lord · ಕುತಃ — from where · ಹ ವಾ — indeed · ಇಮಾಃ — these · ಪ್ರಜಾಃ — creatures / beings · ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತೇ — are born / come into being · ಇತಿ — thus ॥

---

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ಅಥ ಸಂವತ್ಸರಾತ್ ಊರ್ಧ್ವಂ ಕಬಂಧೀ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನಃ ಉಪೇತ್ಯ ಉಪಗಮ್ಯ ಪಪ್ರಚ್ಛ ಪೃಷ್ಟವಾನ್ — ಹೇ ಭಗವನ್, ಕುತಃ ಕಸ್ಮಾತ್ ಹ ವೈ ಇಮಾಃ ಬ್ರಾಹ್ಮಣ-ಆದ್ಯಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತೇ ಉತ್ಪದ್ಯಂತೇ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಅಪರವಿದ್ಯಾಕರ್ಮಣೋಃ ಸಮುಚ್ಚಿತಯೋಃ ಯತ್ಕಾರ್ಯಂ ಯಾ ಗತಿಃ ತದ್ವಕ್ತವ್ಯಮ್ ಇತಿ ತದರ್ಥಃ ಅಯಂ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಃ ॥

### Overall Translation

Then — after the period of one year (spent in the discipline prescribed in verse 2)
— Kabandhi Kātyāyana approached, came near, and asked: "O Revered Sir, from where
indeed are these creatures — Brāhmaṇas and the like — born, come into being?"

The purpose of this question is: what is the fruit and what is the destination of
the lower knowledge (aparavidyā) and action (karma) combined together — that is
what needs to be stated.

### Word-to-Word

ಅಥ — then / after · ಸಂವತ್ಸರಾತ್ ಊರ್ಧ್ವಂ — after the year (of discipline) · ಕಬಂಧೀ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನಃ — Kabandhi Kātyāyana · ಉಪೇತ್ಯ — having approached · ಉಪಗಮ್ಯ — having come near · ಪಪ್ರಚ್ಛ — asked · ಪೃಷ್ಟವಾನ್ — enquired · ಹೇ ಭಗವನ್ — O Revered Sir · ಕುತಃ — from where · ಕಸ್ಮಾತ್ — from what (cause) · ಹ ವೈ — indeed · ಇಮಾಃ — these · ಬ್ರಾಹ್ಮಣಾದ್ಯಾಃ — Brāhmaṇas and the like · ಪ್ರಜಾಃ — creatures / beings · ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತೇ — are born · ಉತ್ಪದ್ಯಂತೇ — come into being · ಇತಿ — thus ।

ಅಪರವಿದ್ಯಾಕರ್ಮಣೋಃ — of lower knowledge and action · ಸಮುಚ್ಚಿತಯೋಃ — combined together · ಯತ್ ಕಾರ್ಯಂ — what the fruit is · ಯಾ ಗತಿಃ — what the destination is · ತತ್ ವಕ್ತವ್ಯಂ — that needs to be stated · ಇತಿ — thus · ತದರ್ಥಃ — the purpose of that · ಅಯಂ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಃ — this question ॥

---

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

The Bhāṣya's second sentence — identifying the real purpose of the question —
requires explicit anvaya, as it is Śaṅkara's interpretive gloss, not a paraphrase
of the Mūla.

---

**Sentence — The real import of Kabandhi's question**

*As written:*
> ಅಪರವಿದ್ಯಾಕರ್ಮಣೋಃ ಸಮುಚ್ಚಿತಯೋಃ ಯತ್ ಕಾರ್ಯಂ ಯಾ ಗತಿಃ
> ತತ್ ವಕ್ತವ್ಯಂ ಇತಿ ತದರ್ಥಃ ಅಯಂ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಃ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> ಅಯಂ ಪ್ರಶ್ನಃ — "ಕುತಃ ಇಮಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತೇ" ಇತಿ —
> ತದರ್ಥಃ [ಏಕಃ] : ಅಪರವಿದ್ಯಾ-ಕರ್ಮಣೋಃ ಸಮುಚ್ಚಿತಯೋಃ
> ಯತ್ ಕಾರ್ಯಂ [ಫಲಂ] ಯಾ ಚ ಗತಿಃ —
> ತತ್ [ಸರ್ವಂ] ವಕ್ತವ್ಯಂ [ಇತ್ಯಭಿಪ್ರಾಯಃ] ।

*English:* This question — "from where are these creatures born?" — has this
purpose: (the questioner is implying) what is the fruit and what is the
destination of lower knowledge and action when combined together — that is what
needs to be stated.

*Note:* Śaṅkara's identification of the question's real purpose (tadarthah) is a
crucial hermeneutical move. The surface question appears to be about cosmogony
(where do creatures come from?). But Śaṅkara identifies the deeper intent:
the question is really about the fruit and destination of aparavidyā-karma-
samuccaya — the combined path of lower knowledge and ritual action. This is what
the entire first Praśna goes on to answer.

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಅನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾಃ ಇತಿ ಉಪಕ್ರಾಂತೇ ಅಸ್ಮಿನ್ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪ್ರಕರಣೇ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿ-ಕರ್ತೃಕ-ಪ್ರಜಾಸೃಷ್ಟಿ-ವಿಷಯ-ಪ್ರಶ್ನ-ಪ್ರತ್ಯುಕ್ತ್ಯೋಃ ಅಸಂಗತಿಂ ಆಶಂಕ್ಯ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ-ಪ್ರತ್ಯುಕ್ತಿ-ರೂಪಾಯಾಃ ಶ್ರುತೇಃ ತಾತ್ಪರ್ಯಂ ಆಹ – ಅಪರವಿದ್ಯಾ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ತೇಷಾಂ ಅಸೌ ವಿರಜಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಲೋಕಃ ಇತಿ ಸಮುಚ್ಚಿತ-ಕಾರ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಲೋಕಸ್ಯ ಅಥ ಉತ್ತರೇಣ ಇತಿ ತದ್ಗತೇಃ ದೇವಯಾನ-ಮಾರ್ಗಸ್ಯ ಚ ಇಹ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಮಾಣತ್ವಾತ್ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಇದಂ ಉಪಲಕ್ಷಣಂ ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮಣಾಂ ಚ ಇತಿ ಅಪಿ ದ್ರಷ್ಟವ್ಯಮ್ ।
>
> ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮ-ಕಾರ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಅಪಿ ಚಂದ್ರಲೋಕಸ್ಯ ತದ್ಗತೇಃ ಪಿತೃಯಾಣಸ್ಯ ಚ "ತೇಷಾಂ ಏವ ಏಷ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಲೋಕಃ" "ಪ್ರಜಾಕಾಮಾ ದಕ್ಷಿಣಂ ಪ್ರತಿಪದ್ಯಂತೇ" ಇತಿ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯಮಾಣತ್ವಾತ್ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಯದ್ಯಪಿ ಇದಂ ಅಪಿ ಪರಬ್ರಹ್ಮ-ಜಿಜ್ಞಾಸಾ-ಅವಸರೇ ಅಸಂಗತಂ ಏವ ತಥಾಪಿ ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮ-ಕಾರ್ಯಾತ್ ಸಮುಚ್ಚಿತಕರ್ಮ-ಕಾರ್ಯಾತ್ ಚ ವಿರಕ್ತಸ್ಯ ಏವ ತತ್ρ ಅಧಿಕಾರಃ ಇತಿ ತತಃ ವೈರಾಗ್ಯ-ಅರ್ಥಂ ಇದಂ ಉಚ್ಯತೇ ।
>
> ಯದ್ಯಪಿ ಮುಖತಃ ಸೃಷ್ಟಿಃ ಪ್ರತೀಯತೇ ತಥಾಪಿ ತದ್-ಉಕ್ತೌ ಪ್ರಯೋಜನ-ಅಭಾವಾತ್ ಸೃಷ್ಟಿ-ಉಕ್ತಿ-ವ್ಯಾಜೇನ ಪರವಿದ್ಯಾ-ಫಲಂ ಏವ ಅತ್ρ ಉಚ್ಯತೇ ಇತಿ ಭಾವಃ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಶ್ನ ಇತಿ ಪ್ರತಿವಚನಂ ಚ ಇತಿ ಅಪಿ ದ್ರಷ್ಟವ್ಯಂ ತಾಭ್ಯಾಂ ಏವ ತದ್-ಉಕ್ತೇಃ ಇತಿ ॥ ೩ ॥

### Overall Translation

Ānandagiri opens by identifying a potential objection: this is a Brahma-prakaraṇa
(a context directed at enquiry into Brahman) — introduced with the words "seeking
the supreme Brahman" (verse 2). So a question and answer about Prajāpati's
creation of creatures would seem incongruous (asaṅgati) here. To resolve this
apparent incongruity, Śaṅkara states the real purport (tātparya) of the
question-and-answer in the form of the Śruti — with the words "aparavidyā" etc.

Ānandagiri then explains the two things that will be stated in the Praśna in
answer to this question:

First: the fruit of the combined (samuccita) path of aparavidyā and karma — namely
the viraja Brahmaloka ("that spotless Brahmaloka belongs to those," verse 16) —
and the path leading to it, the Devayāna (Northern path), will be stated here
(in the course of this Praśna). This is the meaning.

Second: and this is also an upalakaṣaṇa (indicative) for the fruit of ritual
action alone (kevalakamma). The lunar world as the fruit of ritual action alone,
and the Pitṛyāṇa as the path leading to it — these will also be stated here with
the words "this Brahmaloka belongs to those very ones" and "those who desire
progeny resort to the Southern path."

Even though this topic (creation of creatures and the paths) is, strictly speaking,
incongruous in a context of enquiry into the supreme Brahman — nevertheless, only
one who is dispassionate (virakta) towards both the fruit of ritual action alone
and the fruit of combined action has the qualification (adhikāra) for that
(enquiry into Brahman). Therefore this is stated for the purpose of generating
dispassion (vairāgya).

Even though on the surface the creation (of creatures) appears to be what is being
described — nevertheless, since there would be no purpose in stating mere cosmogony,
it is the fruit of higher knowledge (paravidyā-phala) that is being stated here
under the pretext of describing creation. This is the import.

And the words "praśna" (question) and "prativacana" (answer) should also be
understood together — for both the question and the answer serve the same purpose
of stating that fruit.

### Word-to-Word

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | Kabandhi Kātyāyana poses the first question of the Praśna Upaniṣad — from where are creatures born? |
| **ಭಾṣya** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Identifies the time (after one year of discipline); clarifies the scope of "creatures" as Brāhmaṇas and the like; most importantly identifies the real intent of the question as asking about the fruit and destination of aparavidyā-karma-samuccaya |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | Resolves the apparent incongruity of a cosmogony question in a Brahman-enquiry context; identifies the two fruits to be described (viraja Brahmaloka for samuccita-karma; lunar world for kevala-karma); states the real purpose is vairāgya (dispassion); confirms that even the creation narrative is a pretext for stating paravidyā-phala |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **The first question of the Praśna Upaniṣad** — "from where are creatures
   born?" — is deceptively simple. Śaṅkara and Ānandagiri together show it
   carries a much deeper intent.
2. **The real purpose is threefold** according to Ānandagiri: (a) to state the
   fruit and path of the combined knowledge-action path (samuccita — viraja
   Brahmaloka via Devayāna), (b) to state the fruit and path of ritual action
   alone (kevala — lunar world via Pitṛyāṇa), and (c) to generate vairāgya
   (dispassion) towards both, as the qualification for Brahman-enquiry.
3. **Asaṅgati resolved** — the apparent incongruity of discussing creation in
   a Brahman-enquiry context is resolved by Ānandagiri: the creation narrative
   is a vyāja (pretext). The real content being stated under this pretext is
   paravidyā-phala — the fruit of higher knowledge.
4. **Vairāgya-artha** — dispassion as the purpose — is the key hermeneutical
   principle Ānandagiri introduces. One who sees both the lunar world and the
   viraja Brahmaloka as limited fruits will naturally be drawn towards the
   unlimited — the supreme Brahman. The entire first Praśna serves this purpose.
5. **Both question and answer serve the same purpose** — Ānandagiri's closing
   note that "praśna" and "prativacana" together state the fruit is a structural
   observation: the entire dialogue (question + answer) is a single Śruti unit
   aimed at one purpose, not a mere exchange of information.
