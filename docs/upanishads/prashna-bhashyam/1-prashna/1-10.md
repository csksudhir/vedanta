# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೧೦
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 10

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ಅಥೋತ್ತರೇಣ ತಪಸಾ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ ವಿದ್ಯಯಾತ್ಮಾನಮನ್ವಿಷ್ಯಾದಿತ್ಯಮಭಿಜಯಂತೇ ।
>
> ಏತದ್ವೈ ಪ್ರಾಣಾನಾಮಾಯತನಮೇತದಮೃತಮಭಯಮೇತತ್ಪರಾಯಣಮೇತಸ್ಮಾನ್ನ ಪುನರಾವರ್ತಂತ ಇತ್ಯೇಷ ನಿರೋಧಃ ।
>
> ತದೇಷ ಶ್ಲೋಕಃ ॥ ೧೦ ॥

### Overall Translation

Now, by the Northern path — through austerity, celibacy (brahmacarya), faith
(śraddhā), and knowledge (vidyā) — having sought out the Self, they win the Sun
(Āditya).

This (the Sun) is indeed the abode of all the prāṇas, it is immortal, it is
fearless, it is the supreme goal — from it they do not return again. This is the
stopping (nirodha, the end of transmigration).

And on this subject there is the following verse (śloka).

### Word-to-Word

ಅಥ — now / next · ಉತ್ತರೇಣ — by the Northern (path) · ತಪಸಾ — through austerity · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ — through celibacy / brahmacarya · ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ — through faith · ವಿದ್ಯಯಾ — through knowledge · ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ — the Self · ಅನ್ವಿಷ್ಯ — having sought out / having enquired into · ಆದಿತ್ಯಂ — the Sun · ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ — they win / attain ।

ಏತತ್ — this · ವೈ — indeed · ಪ್ರಾಣಾನಾಂ — of the prāṇas (vital forces) · ಆಯತನಂ — the abode / support · ಏತತ್ — this · ಅಮೃತಂ — immortal / deathless · ಅಭಯಂ — fearless / without fear · ಏತತ್ — this · ಪರಾಯಣಂ — the supreme goal / final refuge · ಏತಸ್ಮಾತ್ — from this · ನ — not · ಪುನಃ — again · ಆವರ್ತಂತೇ — they return · ಇತಿ — thus · ಏಷಃ — this · ನಿರೋಧಃ — the stopping / the end of return ।

ತತ್ — on that · ಏಷಃ — this · ಶ್ಲೋಕಃ — verse / śloka ॥

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ಅಥ ಉತ್ತರೇಣ ಅಯನೇನ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತೇರಂಶಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಮತ್ತಾರಮ್ ಆದಿತ್ಯಮ್ ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ ।
>
> ಕೇನ ? ತಪಸಾ ಇಂದ್ರಿಯಜಯೇನ ।
>
> ವಿಶೇಷತೋ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ ವಿದ್ಯಯಾ ಚ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮವಿಷಯಯಾ ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಸೂರ್ಯಂ ಜಗತಃ ತಸ್ಥುಷಶ್ಚ ಅನ್ವಿಷ್ಯ ಅಹಮಸ್ಮೀತಿ ವಿದಿತ್ವಾ ಆದಿತ್ಯಮ್ ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಪ್ನುವಂತಿ ।
>
> ಏತದ್ವೈ ಆಯತನಂ ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಾಣಾನಾಂ ಸಾಮಾನ್ಯಮಾಯತನಮ್ ಆಶ್ರಯಃ ಏತತ್ ಅಮೃತಮ್ ಅವಿನಾಶಿ ಅಭಯಮ್ ಅತ ಏವ ಭಯವರ್ಜಿತಮ್ ನ ಚಂದ್ರವತ್ಕ್ಷಯವೃದ್ಧಿಭಯವತ್ ; ಏತತ್ ಪರಾಯಣಂ ಪರಾ ಗತಿರ್ವಿದ್ಯಾವತಾಂ ಕರ್ಮಿಣಾಂ ಚ ಜ್ಞಾನವತಾಮ್ ಏತಸ್ಮಾನ್ನ ಪುನರಾವರ್ತಂತೇ ಯಥೇತರೇ ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮಿಣ ಇತಿ ಯಸ್ಮಾತ್ ಏಷಃ ಅವಿದುಷಾಂ ನಿರೋಧಃ, ಆದಿತ್ಯಾದ್ಧಿ ನಿರುದ್ಧಾ ಅವಿದ್ವಾಂಸಃ ।
>
> ನೈತೇ ಸಂವತ್ಸರಮಾದಿತ್ಯಮಾತ್ಮಾನಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಮಭಿಪ್ರಾಪ್ನುವಂತಿ ।
>
> ಸ ಹಿ ಸಂವತ್ಸರಃ ಕಾಲಾತ್ಮಾ ಅವಿದುಷಾಂ ನಿರೋಧಃ ।
>
> ತತ್ ತತ್ರಾಸ್ಮಿನ್ನರ್ಥೇ ಏಷಃ ಶ್ಲೋಕಃ ಮಂತ್ರಃ ॥

### Overall Translation

Now by the Northern solstice-path, they win the Sun (Āditya) — that portion of
Prajāpati which is Prāṇa, the eater.

How? Through austerity, which is conquest of the senses.

More specifically, through brahmacarya, through faith, and through knowledge
directed at the identity with Prajāpati's Self — having sought out the Self,
which is Prāṇa, which is the Sun, the support of what moves and what stands still
in the universe, and having realised "I am That (aham asmi)" — they win, they
reach the Sun.

This (Sun) is indeed the common abode, the support of all prāṇas. This is
immortal, imperishable. This is fearless — free from fear precisely because it is
unlike the Moon which is subject to waxing, waning, and the fear of decline. This
is the supreme goal, the highest destination of those who have knowledge alone and
of those who combine action with knowledge. From this they do not return, unlike
those others who are mere performers of ritual action. Therefore this (Sun, the
Year) is the nirodha — the stopping — for the ignorant; for the ignorant are
stopped, held back, by the Sun (Āditya).

These (the ignorant) do not reach the Year, the Sun, the Self, the Prāṇa.

For that Year, whose nature is Time (kālātmā), is the nirodha — the boundary —
for the ignorant.

And on this subject there is this śloka — this mantra.

### Word-to-Word

ಅಥ — now / next · ಉತ್ತರೇಣ ಅಯನೇನ — by the Northern solstice-path · ಪ್ರಜಾಪತೇಃ — of Prajāpati · ಅಂಶಂ — portion / aspect · ಪ್ರಾಣಂ — Prāṇa (the life-force) · ಅತ್ತಾರಂ — the eater / consumer · ಆದಿತ್ಯಂ — the Sun · ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ — they win / attain ।

ಕೇನ — by what (means)? · ತಪಸಾ — through austerity · ಇಂದ್ರಿಯಜಯೇನ — through conquest of the senses ।

ವಿಶೇಷತಃ — more specifically · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ — through brahmacarya (celibacy / student discipline) · ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ — through faith · ವಿದ್ಯಯಾ ಚ — and through knowledge · ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮವಿಷಯಯಾ — directed at / concerned with the identity with Prajāpati's Self · ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ — the Self · ಪ್ರಾಣಂ — Prāṇa · ಸೂರ್ಯಂ — the Sun · ಜಗತಃ — of the world / universe · ತಸ್ಥುಷಃ ಚ — and of what stands still · ಅನ್ವಿಷ್ಯ — having sought out / enquired into · ಅಹಮಸ್ಮಿ ಇತಿ — "I am That" · ವಿದಿತ್ವಾ — having known / realised · ಆದಿತ್ಯಂ — the Sun · ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ — they win · ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಪ್ನುವಂತಿ — they reach / attain ।

ಏತತ್ ವೈ — this indeed · ಆಯತನಂ — abode / support · ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಾಣಾನಾಂ — of all prāṇas · ಸಾಮಾನ್ಯಂ — common / universal · ಆಯತನಂ — abode · ಆಶ್ರಯಃ — support / refuge · ಏತತ್ — this · ಅಮೃತಂ — immortal · ಅವಿನಾಶಿ — imperishable · ಅಭಯಂ — fearless · ಅತ ಏವ — precisely therefore · ಭಯವರ್ಜಿತಂ — free from fear · ನ ಚಂದ್ರವತ್ — unlike the Moon · ಕ್ಷಯವೃದ್ಧಿಭಯವತ್ — subject to waning, waxing, and fear · ಏತತ್ — this · ಪರಾಯಣಂ — the supreme goal · ಪರಾ ಗತಿಃ — the highest destination · ವಿದ್ಯಾವತಾಂ — of those who have knowledge (alone) · ಕರ್ಮಿಣಾಂ ಚ ಜ್ಞಾನವತಾಂ — and of those who combine action with knowledge · ಏತಸ್ಮಾತ್ — from this · ನ ಪುನಃ ಆವರ್ತಂತೇ — they do not return again · ಯಥಾ ಇತರೇ — unlike those others · ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮಿಣಃ — mere performers of ritual action · ಇತಿ — thus · ಯಸ್ಮಾತ್ — because / therefore · ಏಷಃ — this · ಅವಿದುಷಾಂ — for the ignorant · ನಿರೋಧಃ — the stopping / the boundary · ಆದಿತ್ಯಾತ್ ಹಿ — for from the Sun · ನಿರುದ್ಧಾಃ — stopped / held back · ಅವಿದ್ವಾಂಸಃ — the ignorant ।

ನ ಏತೇ — these (ignorant ones) do not · ಸಂವತ್ಸರಂ — the Year · ಆದಿತ್ಯಂ — the Sun · ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ — the Self · ಪ್ರಾಣಂ — Prāṇa · ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಪ್ನುವಂತಿ — reach / attain ।

ಸಃ ಹಿ — for that · ಸಂವತ್ಸರಃ — Year · ಕಾಲಾತ್ಮಾ — whose nature is Time · ಅವಿದುಷಾಂ — for the ignorant · ನಿರೋಧಃ — the stopping / boundary ।

ತತ್ — on that · ತತ್ರ — there / on this subject · ಅಸ್ಮಿನ್ ಅರ್ಥೇ — in this matter · ಏಷಃ — this · ಶ್ಲೋಕಃ — verse · ಮಂತ್ರಃ — mantra ॥

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

The Bhāṣya for this verse has two sentences that require explicit anvaya treatment,
both flagged by Ānandagiri.

---

**Sentence 1 — The means of attaining the Sun**

*As written:*
> ವಿಶೇಷತೋ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ ವಿದ್ಯಯಾ ಚ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮವಿಷಯಯಾ
> ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಸೂರ್ಯಂ ಜಗತಃ ತಸ್ಥುಷಶ್ಚ ಅನ್ವಿಷ್ಯ ಅಹಮಸ್ಮೀತಿ ವಿದಿತ್ವಾ
> ಆದಿತ್ಯಮ್ ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> [ಸಾಧಕಾಃ] ವಿಶೇಷತಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯೇಣ, ಶ್ರದ್ಧಯಾ, ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮ-ವಿಷಯಯಾ ವಿದ್ಯಯಾ ಚ —
> ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ [ಯಃ] ಪ್ರಾಣಃ, [ಯಃ] ಸೂರ್ಯಃ, [ಯಃ] ಜಗತಃ ತಸ್ಥುಷಃ ಚ [ಆಧಾರಃ] —
> ಅನ್ವಿಷ್ಯ, ಅಹಮಸ್ಮಿ ಇತಿ ವಿದಿತ್ವಾ,
> ಆದಿತ್ಯಂ ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ ।

*English:* The seekers — specifically through brahmacarya, through faith, and
through knowledge directed at identity with Prajāpati's Self — having sought out
the Self (which is Prāṇa, which is the Sun, which is the support of the moving
and the still in the universe) and having realised "I am That" — win the Sun.

*Note:* The long participial chain (ಅನ್ವಿಷ್ಯ … ವಿದಿತ್ವಾ) governs the final verb
ಅಭಿಜಯಂತೇ. The relative clause defining ಆತ್ಮಾನಂ as Prāṇa, Sun, and universal
support is embedded mid-sentence and must be separated out. The qualifier
ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮವಿಷಯಯಾ modifies ವಿದ್ಯಯಾ only, not all three instruments.

---

**Sentence 2 — Nirodha: the Sun as boundary for the ignorant**

*As written:*
> ಏತಸ್ಮಾನ್ನ ಪುನರಾವರ್ತಂತೇ ಯಥೇತರೇ ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮಿಣ ಇತಿ ಯಸ್ಮಾತ್
> ಏಷಃ ಅವಿದುಷಾಂ ನಿರೋಧಃ, ಆದಿತ್ಯಾದ್ಧಿ ನಿರುದ್ಧಾ ಅವಿದ್ವಾಂಸಃ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> [ಜ್ಞಾನವತಾಂ] ಏತಸ್ಮಾತ್ [ಆದಿತ್ಯಾತ್] ನ ಪುನಃ ಆವರ್ತಂತೇ —
> [ಆವರ್ತಂತೇ] ಯಥಾ ಇತರೇ ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮಿಣಃ [ಆವರ್ತಂತೇ] ।
>
> ಯಸ್ಮಾತ್ [ಏವಂ] — ಏಷಃ [ಸಂವತ್ಸರಃ / ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ] ಅವಿದುಷಾಂ ನಿರೋಧಃ ।
>
> ಆದಿತ್ಯಾತ್ ಹಿ ಅವಿದ್ವಾಂಸಃ ನಿರುದ್ಧಾಃ [ಭವಂತಿ] ।

*English:* Those with knowledge do not return from the Sun — unlike those others
who are mere ritual performers (who do return). Because this is so — this (Sun /
Year) is the nirodha, the stopping-point, for the ignorant; for the ignorant are
indeed held back, stopped, at the Sun.

*Note:* The sentence pivots on ಯಸ್ಮಾತ್ … ಏಷಃ ನಿರೋಧಃ, where "nirodha" carries a
double meaning: (1) the final stopping of transmigration for the knowers, and (2)
the barrier beyond which the ignorant cannot pass. Ānandagiri clarifies that the
subject of ನಿರುದ್ಧಾ is ಅವಿದ್ವಾಂಸಃ — the ignorant are stopped at the Sun, not
liberated by it.

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ಅಥೇತಿ ।
>
> ಮಾರ್ಗಾಂತರಾರಂಭಾರ್ಥಃ ಅಥಶಬ್ದಃ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮವಿಷಯಾ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ತತ್ತಾದಾತ್ಮ್ಯವಿಷಯಯಾ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಆದಿತ್ಯಮಭಿಜಯಂತೇ ಇತಿ ಪೂರ್ವಮನ್ವಯಾರ್ಥಮುಕ್ತಂ ಇದಾನೀಂ ವ್ಯಾಖ್ಯಾನಾರ್ಥಮಿತಿ ದ್ರಷ್ಟವ್ಯಮ್ ।
>
> ಸಾಮಾನ್ಯಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಸಮಷ್ಟಿರೂಪಮಿತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ವಿದ್ಯಾವತಾಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಕರ್ಮಾನಧಿಕಾರಿಣಾಂ ಅತ ಏವ ಕೇವಲೋಪಾಸನವತಾಮಿತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಕರ್ಮಿಣಾಂ ಚ ಜ್ಞಾನವತಾಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಸಮುಚ್ಚಯವತಾಮಿತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ನನು ಕೇವಲಕರ್ಮಿಣಾಮಪ್ಯಾದಿತ್ಯಪ್ರಾಪ್ತಾವಪುನರಾವೃತ್ತಿರ್ಭವಿಷ್ಯತೀತ್ಯಾಶಂಕ್ಯ ತೇಷಾಮಾದಿತ್ಯಪ್ರಾಪ್ತಿರೇವ ನಾಸ್ತೀತಿ ವಕ್ತುಮಿತ್ಯೇಷ ಇತಿ ವಾಕ್ಯಂ ವ್ಯಾಚಷ್ಟೇ – ಇತಿ ಯಸ್ಮಾದಿತಿ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ತೇಷಾಮಾದಿತ್ಯಪ್ರಾಪ್ತಿರನಾಶಂಕ್ಯೇತಿ ಶೇಷಃ ।
>
> ಯದ್ವಾ ತಸ್ಯಾಯನೇ ಇತ್ಯಾರಭ್ಯ ಇತ್ಯೇಷ ನಿರೋಧ ಇತ್ಯಂತಂ ಶ್ರುತಿವಾಕ್ಯಂ ಅಯನಯೋಃ ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣತ್ವಪ್ರತಿಪಾದನಪರತಯಾ ವ್ಯಾಖ್ಯೇಯಮ್ ।
>
> ತಥಾ ಹಿ ।
>
> ಸಂವತ್ಸರಸ್ಯ ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣಮಿಥುನನಿರ್ವರ್ತ್ಯತ್ವೇ ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣರೂಪತ್ವಂ ಚ ವಕ್ತವ್ಯಂ ತತ್ಕಥಮಿತಿ ಪೃಚ್ಛತಿ – ತತ್ಕಥಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ತದವಯವಯೋಃ ಅಯನಯೋಃ ತದ್ರೂಪತ್ವಂ ವಕ್ತುಂ ತಯೋಃ ಪ್ರಥಮಂ ಪ್ರಸಿದ್ಧಮಾಹ – ತಸ್ಯೇತಿ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಸಿದ್ಧಿಮೇವ ಆಹ – ಯಾಭ್ಯಾಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಏವಮಪಿ ಕಥಂ ತಯೋಃ ತದಾತ್ಮಕತ್ವಮಿತ್ಯಾಹ – ಕಥಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ದಕ್ಷಿಣಾಯನಸ್ಯ ರಯಿತ್ವಂ ವಕ್ತುಂ ಕರ್ಮಿಣಾಂ ರಯಿರೂಪಚಂದ್ರನಿರ್ವರ್ತಕತ್ವಮಾಹ – ತತ್ತತ್ರೇತಿ ।
>
> ಲೋಕಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಸೋಮರೂಪಂ ಶರೀರಮಿತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಯ ಕರ್ಮಕೃತತ್ವಂ ಪುನರಾವೃತ್ತ್ಯಾ ಸಾಧಯತಿ – ಕೃತರೂಪತ್ವಾದಿತಿ ।
>

### Overall Translation

Ānandagiri opens by noting that the word "atha" (now/next) signals the beginning
of a new path — the Northern solstice-path — in contrast to the Southern path
just described.

On the phrase "ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮವಿಷಯಯಾ" (knowledge directed at the Self of Prajāpati):
Ānandagiri clarifies this means knowledge directed at identity with that (Prajāpati)
— i.e., the realisation of non-difference (tādātmya).

On the phrase "they win the Sun" — Ānandagiri notes that the first occurrence of
this phrase (in verse 9's context) was for the purpose of establishing the
syntactic connection (anvaya); now in the Bhāṣya it appears again for the purpose
of full explanation (vyākhyāna). These two uses should be distinguished.

On "sāmānya" (common/universal): Ānandagiri explains this means the Sun is the
collective / aggregate form (samaṣṭirūpa) — i.e., the sum total of all prāṇas,
not just one individual prāṇa.

On "vidyāvatām" (those who have knowledge): Ānandagiri explains this refers to
those who are not qualified for ritual action — and precisely therefore are those
who possess only upāsanā (meditation/contemplation) without the combination of
ritual and knowledge.

On "karmiṇāṃ ca jñānavatām" (of those who combine action with knowledge):
Ānandagiri explains this means those who have the combination (samuccaya) — i.e.,
those who unite both ritual action and knowledge.

Ānandagiri then raises and resolves an objection: one might ask — would not even
the mere ritual performers (kevalakarmins) attain non-return once they reach the
Sun? To forestall this, Ānandagiri explains that Śaṅkara's sentence beginning
"iti yasmāt" establishes that those people do not even reach the Sun at all.
Therefore, the question of their non-return from the Sun does not even arise. The
supplementary meaning is: "therefore, their (the kevalakarmins') attaining the
Sun is not to be imagined."

Alternatively, Ānandagiri offers a second interpretation: the entire Śruti passage
from "tasyāyane" (his two paths) through "eṣa nirodhaḥ" (this is the stopping)
should be understood as aimed at establishing that the two ayanas are of the nature
of Rayi and Prāṇa respectively.

For this — the Bhāṣya must state that the Year is produced by the Rayi-Prāṇa pair
and is itself of the nature of Rayi and Prāṇa. The question "how so?" then arises.

To establish the Rayi-Prāṇa nature of the two ayanas — first the well-known fact
about them is stated with "tasya" (his two paths). The well-known fact itself is
then stated with "yābhyām" (by which two). Then the question of how even so the
two ayanas can be of the nature of Rayi and Prāṇa is raised with "katham?" (how?).

To establish that the Southern solstice is of the nature of Rayi — Śaṅkara states
that for the ritual performers, the Moon (which is of the nature of Rayi) is
produced (via the Southern path) — with "tat tatra." The word "lokam" means a body
of the lunar nature (somarūpaṃ śarīram). That this body is produced by action is
established through the fact of rebirth — with "kṛtarūpatvāt."

To establish that the Southern solstice is included within (antarabhāva) the
Rayi-natured Moon (since that Moon is attained via the Southern path), Śaṅkara
states that the Moon is attainable through action — with "yasmāt." And thus the
Rayi-nature of the Southern solstice is established — with "eṣa" (this). The
phrase "pitṛyāṇopalakaṣita" means "that which is attained through the Pitṛyāṇa"
— and therefore the solstice that is its qualifier is also of the nature of Rayi.

Now, the Prāṇa-nature of the Northern solstice is stated with "atha" (now/next).
Since the Northern solstice leads to the Prāṇa-natured Sun, it too is of the
nature of Prāṇa. Thus the Year's being of the nature of the Rayi-Prāṇa pair is
established, and it is reasonable that the Year should be a product (kārya) of
that pair.

The difference between the Sun (attained by the Northern path) and the Moon
(attained by action / the Southern path) is then stated with "etadvai" (this
indeed). Everything else (the remaining analysis) is the same (as what was already
covered).

Finally, on "asmin arthe" (in this matter): Ānandagiri explains this means "in
the matter of the nature of the Saṃvatsara (Year)" — i.e., the śloka to follow
is a Vedic verse that corroborates the entire analysis of the Year as Rayi-Prāṇa.

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | States the Northern path — through austerity, brahmacarya, faith, and knowledge — by which the knower wins the Sun and does not return |
| **ಭಾಷ್ಯ** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Unpacks the means (austerity as sense-conquest; knowledge as realisation of identity with Prajāpati's Self); distinguishes the Sun as immortal, fearless, and the supreme goal from the Moon; explains nirodha as the Sun's dual function — liberation for knowers, a barrier for the ignorant |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | Clarifies atha as marking the new path; tādātmya as the content of vidyā; samaṣṭirūpa as the meaning of sāmānya; resolves the objection about kevalakarmins and non-return; offers two alternative interpretations of the entire Śruti passage; establishes the Prāṇa-nature of the Northern solstice in parallel with the Rayi-nature of the Southern solstice already shown in verse 9 |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **The Northern path** (Uttarāyaṇa / Devayāna) is for those who combine
   austerity, brahmacarya, faith, and knowledge — specifically knowledge of
   identity with Prajāpati's Self (aham asmi — "I am That").
2. **The Sun (Āditya)** is the Prāṇa-aspect of Prajāpati — immortal, fearless,
   the common ground (samaṣṭi) of all prāṇas — in contrast to the Moon which is
   finite, waxing and waning, and subject to decay.
3. **Nirodha (stopping)** operates at two levels: for the knower it is the final
   end of transmigration; for the ignorant the Sun functions as a barrier — they
   are held back at the Sun and do not even reach it, let alone transcend it.
4. **Ānandagiri's key contribution** here is the systematic parallel: just as
   verse 9 established the Rayi-nature of the Southern solstice (through the
   Moon), verse 10 establishes the Prāṇa-nature of the Northern solstice
   (through the Sun) — completing the proof that the Year as a whole is of the
   nature of the Rayi-Prāṇa pair.
5. **The word "atha"** is not merely transitional — Ānandagiri shows it
   structurally marks the shift from the Rayi-analysis to the Prāṇa-analysis,
   mirroring the structure of Prajāpati's dyadic nature.
