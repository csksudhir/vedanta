# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೧
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 1

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ಸುಕೇಶಾ ಚ ಭಾರದ್ವಾಜಃ ಶೈಬ್ಯಶ್ಚ ಸತ್ಯಕಾಮಃ ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣೀ ಚ ಗಾರ್ಗ್ಯಃ ಕೌಸಲ್ಯಶ್ಚಾಶ್ವಲಾಯನೋ ಭಾರ್ಗವೋ ವೈದರ್ಭಿಃ ಕಬಂಧೀ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನಃ ।
>
> ತೇ ಹೈತೇ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪರಾ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಾನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾ ಏಷ ಹ ವೈ ತತ್ಸರ್ವಂ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯತೀತಿ ।
>
> ತೇ ಹ ಸಮಿತ್ಪಾಣಯೋ ಭಗವಂತಂ ಪಿಪ್ಪಲಾದಮುಪಸನ್ನಾಃ ॥ ೧ ॥

### Overall Translation

Sukeśā Bhāradvāja, Śaibya Satyakāma, Sauryāyaṇī Gārgya, Kausalya Āśvalāyana,
Bhārgava Vaidarbhi, and Kabandhi Kātyāyana —

These indeed, devoted to Brahman, established in Brahman, seeking the supreme
Brahman (thinking) "he will tell us all of that" —

They, with fuel-sticks in hand, approached the revered Pippalāda.

### Word-to-Word

ಸುಕೇಶಾ ಚ — Sukeśā (personal name) and · ಭಾರದ್ವಾಜಃ — (son of) Bharadvāja (patronymic) · ಶೈಬ್ಯಃ ಚ — and Śaibya (son of Śibi) · ಸತ್ಯಕಾಮಃ — Satyakāma (personal name) · ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣೀ — Sauryāyaṇī (grandson of Sūrya, Vedic form) · ಚ — and · ಗಾರ್ಗ್ಯಃ — Gārgya (of the Garga lineage) · ಕೌಸಲ್ಯಃ ಚ — and Kausalya (personal name) · ಆಶ್ವಲಾಯನಃ — Āśvalāyana (son of Aśvala) · ಭಾರ್ಗವಃ — Bhārgava (of the Bhṛgu lineage) · ವೈದರ್ಭಿಃ — Vaidarbhi (born in Vidarbha) · ಕಬಂಧೀ — Kabandhi (personal name) · ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನಃ — Kātyāyana (son of Katya, youthful suffix) ।

ತೇ ಹ ಏತೇ — these indeed · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪರಾಃ — devoted to Brahman / having Brahman as the supreme goal · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ — established in Brahman / steadfast in its practice · ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ — the supreme Brahman · ಅನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾಃ — seeking / enquiring after · ಏಷ ಹ ವೈ — this one indeed · ತತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ — all of that · ವಕ್ಷ್ಯತಿ — will tell / will declare · ಇತಿ — thinking thus ।

ತೇ ಹ — they indeed · ಸಮಿತ್ಪಾಣಯಃ — with fuel-sticks in hand · ಭಗವಂತಂ — the revered one · ಪಿಪ್ಪಲಾದಂ — Pippalāda · ಉಪಸನ್ನಾಃ — approached / came near ॥

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ಸುಕೇಶಾ ಚ ನಾಮತಃ, ಭರದ್ವಾಜಸ್ಯ ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಭಾರದ್ವಾಜಃ ।
>
> ಶೈಬ್ಯಃ ಚ ಶಿಬೇಃ ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಶೈಬ್ಯಃ, ಸತ್ಯಕಾಮಃ ನಾಮತಃ ।
>
> ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣೀ ಸೂರ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಸೌರ್ಯಃ, ತಸ್ಯ ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣಿಃ ; ಛಾಂದಸಂ ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣೀ ಇತಿ ; ಗಾರ್ಗ್ಯಃ ಗರ್ಗ-ಗೋತ್ರ-ಉತ್ಪನ್ನಃ ।
>
> ಕೌಸಲ್ಯಃ ಚ ನಾಮತಃ, ಅಶ್ವಲಸ್ಯ ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಆಶ್ವಲಾಯನಃ ।
>
> ಭಾರ್ಗವಃ ಭೃಗೋಃ ಗೋತ್ರ-ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಭಾರ್ಗವಃ, ವೈದರ್ಭಿಃ ವಿದರ್ಭೇಷು ಭವಃ ।
>
> ಕಬಂಧೀ ನಾಮತಃ, ಕತ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಅಪತ್ಯಂ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನಃ ; ವಿದ್ಯಮಾನಃ ಪ್ರಪಿತಾಮಹಃ ಯಸ್ಯ ಸಃ ; ಯುವಪ್ರತ್ಯಯಃ ।
>
> ತೇ ಹ ಏತೇ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪರಾಃ ಅಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಪರತ್ವೇನ ಗತಾಃ, ತದನುಷ್ಠಾನ-ನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ ಚ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ, ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಅನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾಃ ।
>
> ಕಿಂ ತತ್ ಯತ್ ನಿತ್ಯಂ ವಿಜ್ಞೇಯಂ ಇತಿ ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತ್ಯರ್ಥಂ ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ ಯತಿಷ್ಯಾಮಃ ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ತದನ್ವೇಷಣಂ ಕುರ್ವಂತಃ, ತದಧಿಗಮಾಯ ಏಷ ಹ ವೈ ತತ್ಸರ್ವಂ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯತಿ ಇತಿ ಆಚಾರ್ಯಂ ಉಪಜಗ್ಮುಃ ।
>
> ಕಥಮ್ ? ತೇ ಹ ಸಮಿತ್ಪಾಣಯಃ ಸಮಿದ್ಭಾರ-ಗೃಹೀತ-ಹಸ್ತಾಃ ಸಂತಃ, ಭಗವಂತಂ ಪೂಜಾವಂತಂ ಪಿಪ್ಪಲಾದಂ ಆಚಾರ್ಯಂ ಉಪಸನ್ನಾಃ ಉಪಜಗ್ಮುಃ ॥

### Overall Translation

Sukeśā — by name — is the son of Bharadvāja and (is called) Bhāradvāja. Śaibya
is the son of Śibi; Satyakāma is his personal name. Sauryāyaṇī: the son of Sūrya
is Saurya; his son is Sauryāyaṇi — Sauryāyaṇī is the Vedic (chāndasa) form of
this; Gārgya is born in the Garga lineage. Kausalya by personal name; Āśvalāyana
is the son of Aśvala. Bhārgava is the lineage-descendant of Bhṛgu; Vaidarbhi is
one born in Vidarbha. Kabandhi by personal name; Kātyāyana is the son of Katya —
one whose great-grandfather is still living; this is the youthful suffix (yuvapratyaya).

These indeed — brahmaparāḥ: those for whom the lower Brahman (aparabrahma) is the
supreme goal, and brahmaniṣṭhāḥ: those steadfast in its practice — seeking the
supreme Brahman (thinking): "what is it that is eternal and to be known? For its
attainment we shall strive as we wish" — performing that search, (thinking) "this
one will indeed tell us all of that" — they approached the teacher.

How? They, with their hands holding bundles of fuel-sticks, approached the revered
Pippalāda — the teacher worthy of honour.

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

The Bhāṣya's central passage — describing the students' inner state and motivation
— requires explicit anvaya as it contains a complex nested structure.

---

**Sentence — The three-fold description of the students and their purpose**

*As written:*
> ತೇ ಹ ಏತೇ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪರಾಃ ಅಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಪರತ್ವೇನ ಗತಾಃ,
> ತದನುಷ್ಠಾನ-ನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ ಚ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ, ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಅನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾಃ —
> ಕಿಂ ತತ್ ಯತ್ ನಿತ್ಯಂ ವಿಜ್ಞೇಯಂ ಇತಿ ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತ್ಯರ್ಥಂ ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ ಯತಿಷ್ಯಾಮಃ
> ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ತದನ್ವೇಷಣಂ ಕುರ್ವಂತಃ, ತದಧಿಗಮಾಯ ಏಷ ಹ ವೈ ತತ್ಸರ್ವಂ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯತಿ
> ಇತಿ ಆಚಾರ್ಯಂ ಉಪಜಗ್ಮುಃ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> [ಸ್ಥಿತಿ ೧:] ತೇ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪರಾಃ — ಅಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಪರತ್ವೇನ ಗತಾಃ [ಸಂತಃ] ।
>
> [ಸ್ಥಿತಿ ೨:] [ತೇ ಏವ] ಬ್ರಹ್ಮನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ — ತದನುಷ್ಠಾನ-ನಿಷ್ಠಾಃ [ಸಂತಃ] ।
>
> [ಸ್ಥಿತಿ ೩:] [ತೇ ಏವ] ಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಅನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾಃ —
>   "ಕಿಂ ತತ್ ಯತ್ ನಿತ್ಯಂ ವಿಜ್ಞೇಯಂ?
>   ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತ್ಯರ್ಥಂ ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ ಯತಿಷ್ಯಾಮಃ" ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ತದನ್ವೇಷಣಂ ಕುರ್ವಂತಃ ।
>
> [ಅತಃ:] "ಏಷ ಹ ವೈ ತತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ ವಕ್ಷ್ಯತಿ" ಇತಿ [ನಿಶ್ಚಿತ್ಯ] —
> ತದಧಿಗಮಾಯ ಆಚಾರ್ಯಂ ಉಪಜಗ್ಮುಃ ।

*English:* Those students were in three states simultaneously — [1] brahmaparāḥ:
having adopted the lower Brahman as their supreme goal; [2] brahmaniṣṭhāḥ:
steadfast in its practice; [3] seeking the supreme Brahman — performing that
search (thinking: "What is it that is eternal and to be known? For its attainment
we shall strive as we wish"). Having resolved (thinking) "this one will indeed tell
us all of that" — they approached the teacher for the attainment of that.

*Note:* The three descriptions — brahmaparāḥ, brahmaniṣṭhāḥ, and anveṣamāṇāḥ —
are not synonyms. Ānandagiri distinguishes them precisely: brahmaparāḥ refers to
the aparabrahman being their current supreme goal; brahmaniṣṭhāḥ refers to their
steadfast practice of it; anveṣamāṇāḥ refers to their active search for the
parabrahman beyond it.

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣೀ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣಿಃ ಇತಿ ವಕ್ತವ್ಯೇ ದೈರ್ಘ್ಯಂ ಛಾಂದಸಂ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಯುವಪ್ರತ್ಯಯ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಕತ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಯುವ-ಅಪತ್ಯೇ ವಿವಕ್ಷಿತೇ ಫಕ್-ಪ್ರತ್ಯಯೇ ತಸ್ಯ ಆಯನ್-ಆದೇಶೇ ಚ ಕಾತ್ಯಾಯನ ಇತಿ ಸಿಧ್ಯತಿ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಪರಾಣಾಂ ಪುನಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ-ಅನ್ವೇಷಣಂ ಅಯುಕ್ತಂ ಇತ್ಯತಃ ಆಹ – ಅಪರಂ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ನನು ಅಪರ-ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ-ಅನ್ವೇಷಣೇನ ಏವ ಪುರುಷಾರ್ಥ-ಸಿದ್ಧೇಃ ಕಿಂ ಪರ-ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ-ಅನ್ವೇಷಣೇನ ಇತ್ಯಾಶಂಕತೇ – ಕಿಂ ತತ್ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಯ ಕಃ ಅತಿಶಯಃ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಯ ಅನಿತ್ಯತ್ವೇನ ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತೇಃ ಅಪಿ ಅನಿತ್ಯ-ಹೇತುತ್ವೇನ ಅಪುರುಷಾರ್ಥತ್ವಾತ್ ಪರಸ್ಯ ಏವ ನಿತ್ಯತ್ವಾತ್ ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತೇಃ ತಜ್ಜ್ಞಾನ-ಮಾತ್ರ-ಸಾಧ್ಯತ್ವೇನ ಅಪಿ ನಿತ್ಯತ್ವಾತ್ ಚ ತಸ್ಯ ಏವ ಅನ್ವೇಷಣೀಯತ್ವಂ ಇತಿ ಪರ-ಸ್ವರೂಪ-ಕಥನೇನ ಆಹ – ಯತ್ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಪರ-ಬ್ರಹ್ಮ-ಅನ್ವೇಷಮಾಣಾನಾಂ ಕಃ ಅತಿಶಯಃ ಇತ್ಯತಃ ಆಹ – ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತ್ಯರ್ಥಂ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ತತ್ಪ್ರಾಪ್ತ್ಯರ್ಥೇ ತದಧಿಗಮಾಯ ತದನ್ವೇಷಣಂ ಕುರ್ವಂತಃ ಯಥಾಕಾಮಂ ಯತಿಷ್ಯಾಮಃ ಇತ್ಯೇವಂ ಅಭಿಪ್ರಾಯೇಣ ಇತಿ ಅನ್ವಯಃ ।
>
> ಸಮಿತ್ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಸಮಿದ್ಗ್ರಹಣಂ ಯಥಾಯೋಗ್ಯಂ ದಂತಕಾಷ್ಠ-ಆದಿ-ಉಪಹಾರ-ಉಪಲಕ್ಷಣ-ಅರ್ಥಂ ॥ ೧ ॥

### Overall Translation

On "Sauryāyaṇī": Ānandagiri notes that though the grammatically expected form is
"Sauryāyaṇiḥ" (short final vowel), the long vowel (dīrgha) in "Sauryāyaṇī" is a
Vedic (chāndasa) usage — permitted in the Vedic register.

On "yuvapratyaya" (youthful suffix): Ānandagiri explains the grammar. When the
intent is to denote the youthful descendant (yuva-apatya) of Katya, the suffix
"phak" is applied; then the "āyan" substitution of that suffix yields "Kātyāyana."
This is how the form is derived.

Ānandagiri then raises an objection: if these students are already brahmaparāḥ
(devoted to Brahman), their further seeking of Brahman would be redundant. To
resolve this, Śaṅkara specifies "aparaṃ brahma" — the lower Brahman — with the
words "aparaṃ brahma."

A further objection is raised: if the attainment of the puruṣārtha (human goal)
is already achieved through the search for aparabrahman alone, what is the need for
seeking parabrahman? — with the words "kiṃ tat (what is that?)."

The meaning is: what is the superiority (atiśaya) of parabrahman? Ānandagiri
answers: because aparabrahman is impermanent (anitya), even its attainment is
a cause of impermanence and therefore not the true puruṣārtha. Only parabrahman
is eternal; and since its attainment — which is achieved by knowledge alone — is
also eternal, only it is worthy of search. Śaṅkara states this by describing the
nature of the supreme — with the word "yat (which)."

To the objection — what is the superiority of those seeking parabrahman? —
Ānandagiri answers with the words "tatprāptyartham (for the purpose of its
attainment)." The anvaya is: those who are performing that search (thinking)
"we shall strive as we wish for the attainment of it, for the realisation of it"
— with this intent (they approached the teacher).

On "samit" (fuel-sticks): Ānandagiri notes that the mention of fuel-sticks is an
upalakaṣaṇa (indicative term) for all the appropriate offerings to a teacher —
such as tooth-cleaning sticks (dantakāṣṭha) and the like — whatever is fitting.

### Word-to-Word

ಸೌರ್ಯಾಯಣೀ ಇತಿ — on the word "Sauryāyaṇī" ।

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | Introduces the six students by name, describes their qualification and intention, and records their approach to Pippalāda with fuel-sticks |
| **ಭಾṣya** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Gives the etymologies of all six names; distinguishes brahmaparāḥ (devoted to aparabrahman) from brahmanisṭhāḥ (steadfast in its practice) and anveṣamāṇāḥ (seeking parabrahman); explains the students' inner reasoning; glosses samitpāṇayaḥ |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | Explains the chāndasa form of Sauryāyaṇī; derives Kātyāyana through Pāṇinian grammar (phak + āyan); resolves the apparent redundancy of brahmaparāḥ seeking Brahman; establishes the superiority of parabrahman over aparabrahman through the nitya/anitya distinction; identifies samit as upalakaṣaṇa |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **The six students are qualified seekers** — not beginners. They are already
   brahmaparāḥ (devoted to the lower Brahman) and brahmaniṣṭhāḥ (established
   in its practice). They come to Pippalāda for what lies beyond.
2. **The aparabrahman / parabrahman distinction** is introduced here at the very
   opening of the Upaniṣad. Ānandagiri makes it philosophically precise: aparabrahman
   is impermanent — its attainment is also impermanent and therefore not the true
   puruṣārtha. Parabrahman alone is eternal; its attainment through knowledge
   alone is also eternal. Therefore only parabrahman is worthy of search.
3. **Brahmaparāḥ ≠ brahmanisṭhāḥ ≠ anveṣamāṇāḥ** — three distinct descriptions,
   not synonyms. This precision at the opening sets the interpretive standard for
   the entire Upaniṣad.
4. **Samit as upalakaṣaṇa** — the fuel-sticks stand for all offerings appropriate
   to approaching a teacher (dantakāṣṭha etc.). This is a minor but characteristic
   example of Ānandagiri's method of expanding the scope of a Śruti term.
5. **The frame is set** — the opening verse establishes who the students are, what
   their qualification is, what they are seeking, and how they approached. Everything
   that follows in the six Praśnas flows from this opening disposition.
