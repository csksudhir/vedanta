# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೮
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 8

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ ಹರಿಣಂ ಜಾತವೇದಸಂ ಪರಾಯಣಂ ಜ್ಯೋತಿರೇಕಂ ತಪಂತಮ್ ।
>
> ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಶತಧಾ ವರ್ತಮಾನಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಮುದಯತ್ಯೇಷ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ॥ ೮ ॥

### Overall Translation

All-formed, the golden one, the one who knows all that is born, the supreme
refuge, the one light, shining —

With a thousand rays, existing in a hundredfold way — this Sun rises as the Prāṇa
of all creatures.

### Word-to-Word

ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ — all-formed / of universal form · ಹರಿಣಂ — the golden one / the one with rays · ಜಾತವೇದಸಂ — the one who knows all that is born / omniscient · ಪರಾಯಣಂ — the supreme refuge / the final goal · ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ — the light · ಏಕಂ — the one / without a second · ತಪಂತಂ — shining / heating ।

ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ — having a thousand rays · ಶತಧಾ — in a hundredfold way · ವರ್ತಮಾನಃ — existing / present · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — the Prāṇa (life-force) · ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ — of all creatures / beings · ಉದಯತಿ — rises · ಏಷಃ — this · ಸೂರ್ಯಃ — the Sun ॥

---

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ ಸರ್ವರೂಪಂ ಹರಿಣಂ ರಶ್ಮಿವಂತಂ ಜಾತವೇದಸಂ ಜಾತಪ್ರಜ್ಞಾನಂ ಪರಾಯಣಂ ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಾಣಾಶ್ರಯಂ ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಾಣಿನಾಂ ಚಕ್ಷುರ್ಭೂತಂ ಏಕಂ ಅದ್ವಿತೀಯಂ ತಪಂತಂ ತಾಪಕ್ರಿಯಾಂ ಕುರ್ವಾಣಂ ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಾನಂ ಸೂರ್ಯಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾತವಂತಃ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮವಿದಃ ।
>
> ಕಃ ಅಸೌ ಯಂ ವಿಜ್ಞಾತವಂತಃ ?
>
> ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಅನೇಕರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಶತಧಾ ಅನೇಕಧಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಿಭೇದೇನ ವರ್ತಮಾನಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ ಉದಯತಿ ಏಷಃ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ॥

### Overall Translation

Those who have known the Sun as their own Self — having known him as:
all-formed (of universal form), the golden one (possessed of rays), the
omniscient one (jātavedas — who knows all that is born), the supreme refuge
(support of all prāṇas), the one light (which has become the eye of all
living beings), without a second, and shining (performing the act of
heating) — those are the knowers of Brahman.

Who is that one whom they have known?

He who has a thousand rays, who exists in a hundredfold way — in the
diversity of all living beings — he is the Prāṇa of all creatures: this
Sun rises.

### Word-to-Word

ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ — all-formed / of universal form · ಸರ್ವರೂಪಂ — having all forms · ಹರಿಣಂ — the golden one · ರಶ್ಮಿವಂತಂ — possessed of rays · ಜಾತವೇದಸಂ — the omniscient one / the one who knows all that is born · ಜಾತಪ್ರಜ್ಞಾನಂ — whose knowledge is born / fully arisen · ಪರಾಯಣಂ — the supreme refuge · ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಾಣ-ಆಶ್ರಯಂ — the support / refuge of all prāṇas · ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ — the light · ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಾಣಿನಾಂ — of all living beings · ಚಕ್ಷುರ್ಭೂತಂ — that which has become the eye · ಏಕಂ — one · ಅದ್ವಿತೀಯಂ — without a second · ತಪಂತಂ — shining / heating · ತಾಪಕ್ರಿಯಾಂ ಕುರ್ವಾಣಂ — performing the act of heating · ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಾನಂ — as their own Self · ಸೂರ್ಯಂ — the Sun · ವಿಜ್ಞಾತವಂತಃ — those who have known / realised · ಬ್ರಹ್ಮವಿದಃ — the knowers of Brahman ।

ಕಃ ಅಸೌ — who is that one · ಯಂ — whom · ವಿಜ್ಞಾತವಂತಃ — they have known ।

ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ — having a thousand rays · ಅನೇಕರಶ್ಮಿಃ — having countless rays · ಶತಧಾ — in a hundredfold way · ಅನೇಕಧಾ — in a manifold way · ಪ್ರಾಣಿಭೇದೇನ — in the diversity of living beings · ವರ್ತಮಾನಃ — existing / present · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — the Prāṇa · ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ — of all creatures · ಉದಯತಿ — rises · ಏಷಃ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ — this Sun ॥

---

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

The Bhāṣya for this verse requires explicit anvaya treatment because the Mūla
is a verse (śloka) in which a series of accusative compounds (in the second
case — dvitīyā) cannot be directly connected syntactically to the nominative
sentence that follows. Ānandagiri identifies this as the structural problem
that necessitates the Bhāṣya's approach.

---

**The grammatical problem — accusatives without a verb**

*As written in the Mūla:*
> ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ ಹರಿಣಂ ಜಾತವೇದಸಂ ಪರಾಯಣಂ ಜ್ಯೋತಿರೇಕಂ ತಪಂತಮ್ [accusatives] ।
>
> ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಶತಧಾ ವರ್ತಮಾನಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ ಉದಯತಿ ಏಷ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ [nominatives + verb] ।

*The problem:* The words in the first half — viśvarūpam, hariṇam, jātavedasam,
parāyaṇam, jyotiḥ ekam, tapantam — are all accusative. But there is no verb
in the first half that governs them. The second half states "this Sun rises as
the Prāṇa of creatures" — nominatives with a verb. The two halves cannot be
directly connected as a single sentence (samānādhikaraṇa anvaya would fail).

*Śaṅkara's solution — adhyāhāra (supplying an implied element):*
Śaṅkara supplies the implied verb and object: "those who have known
(vijñātavantaḥ) the Sun as their own Self (svātmānam)" — thereby making the
accusatives the object of the supplied verb, and the knowers of Brahman
(brahmvidaḥ) the subject.

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> [ಯೇ] ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಾನಂ ಸೂರ್ಯಂ [—] ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ, ಹರಿಣಂ, ಜಾತವೇದಸಂ, ಪರಾಯಣಂ,
> ಜ್ಯೋತಿಃ ಏಕಂ ಅದ್ವಿತೀಯಂ, ತಪಂತಂ [—] ವಿಜ್ಞಾತವಂತಃ,
> ತೇ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮವಿದಃ ।
>
> ಕಃ ಅಸೌ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ? [ಯಃ] ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಶತಧಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಿಭೇದೇನ ವರ್ತಮಾನಃ —
> ಏಷಃ ಪ್ರಜಾನಾಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಸೂರ್ಯಃ ಉದಯತಿ ।

*English:* Those who have known the Sun as their own Self — (the Sun who is)
all-formed, the golden one, the omniscient, the supreme refuge, the one
undivided light, the shining one — those are the knowers of Brahman. And who
is that Sun? He who has a thousand rays, who exists in a hundredfold way in
the diversity of living beings — this Sun, the Prāṇa of all creatures, rises.

*Note:* The technical term used by Ānandagiri is **adhyāhāra** — the supply of
an implied element not present in the text, which is necessary to make the
sentence syntactically complete. The second occurrence of "sūryam" and the
supplied "vijñātavantaḥ brahmvidaḥ" are both adhyāhāra. Ānandagiri describes
the problem precisely: the dvitīyā-anta (accusative) compounds cannot have
samānādhikaraṇa (appositional) connection with the prathamā-anta (nominative)
words — hence the vākyabheda (sentence-splitting) and adhyāhāra are the only
solution.

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿ ದ್ವಿತೀಯಾಂತಾನಾಂ ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿ ಪ್ರಥಮಾಂತಾನಾಂ ಸಾಮಾನಾಧಿಕರಣ್ಯೇನ ಅನ್ವಯ-ಅಯೋಗಾತ್ ಅಧ್ಯಾಹಾರಂ ಕೃತ್ವಾ ವಾಕ್ಯಭೇದೇನ ವ್ಯಾಚಷ್ಟೇ – ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಾನಂ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿನಾ ॥ ೮ ॥

### Overall Translation

Ānandagiri's Ṭīkā for this verse is a single compact grammatical observation.
He identifies the structural problem of the verse precisely: the accusative
compounds beginning with "viśvarūpam" (viśvarūpam, hariṇam, jātavedasam,
parāyaṇam, jyotiḥ ekam, tapantam) cannot be connected in a samānādhikaraṇa
(appositional) relationship with the nominative words beginning with
"sahasraraśmiḥ" (sahasraraśmiḥ, śatadhā vartamānaḥ, prāṇaḥ, eṣa sūryaḥ)
— because accusatives and nominatives cannot be in apposition. Therefore,
Śaṅkara makes an adhyāhāra (supplies an implied element) and explains by
splitting the verse into two sentences (vākyabheda) — beginning with
"svātmānam" (as their own Self).

### Word-to-Word

ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಂ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿ — the words beginning with "viśvarūpam" · ದ್ವಿತೀಯಾಂತಾನಾಂ — of those ending in the accusative (dvitīyā / second case) · ಸಹಸ್ರರಶ್ಮಿಃ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿ — the words beginning with "sahasraraśmiḥ" · ಪ್ರಥಮಾಂತಾನಾಂ — of those ending in the nominative (prathamā / first case) · ಸಾಮಾನಾಧಿಕರಣ್ಯೇನ — by appositional connection (samānādhikaraṇa) · ಅನ್ವಯ-ಅಯೋಗಾತ್ — because of the impossibility of syntactic connection · ಅಧ್ಯಾಹಾರಂ ಕೃತ್ವಾ — having made an adhyāhāra (supplied an implied element) · ವಾಕ್ಯಭೇದೇನ — by splitting into two sentences · ವ್ಯಾಚಷ್ಟೇ — (Śaṅkara) explains · ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮಾನಂ ಇತ್ಯಾದಿನಾ — beginning with the words "svātmānam (as their own Self)" ॥

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | Closing verse of the second Praśna — a śloka identifying the Sun as the Prāṇa of all creatures, with a series of epithets and a rising-statement |
| **ಭಾṣya** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Resolves the grammatical problem by adhyāhāra — supplying "vijñātavantaḥ brahmvidaḥ" and "svātmānam" to connect the accusatives; identifies the knowers of Brahman as those who know the Sun as their own Self; glosses each epithet |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | Identifies the grammatical problem precisely — accusative-nominative apposition is impossible (samānādhikaraṇya anvayāyogāt) — and confirms Śaṅkara's solution of adhyāhāra plus vākyabheda |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **This verse is a śloka** — a formal verse in the Upaniṣad serving as
   scriptural authority for the identification of the Sun as Prāṇa, which
   is the central teaching of the second Praśna.
2. **The Sun's seven epithets** form a cumulative portrait of the Prāṇa-as-Sun:
   all-formed (universal), golden (luminous), omniscient (jātavedas), supreme
   refuge, the one undivided light, shining — culminating in the identification
   of this one with the Prāṇa of all creatures.
3. **"Svātmānam sūryam vijñātavantaḥ"** — knowing the Sun as one's own Self —
   is the liberating knowledge. This echoes the "aham asmi" (I am That) of the
   first Praśna's Northern path.
4. **The grammatical teaching** — adhyāhāra and vākyabheda — is as important
   as the philosophical teaching here. The method of resolving verses where
   grammatical connection is impossible is a standard tool of Vedāntic
   commentary, and Ānandagiri names it precisely.
5. **The verse closes the second Praśna** on the same note that the first
   Praśna closed — the Sun as Prāṇa, the supreme light, the knower's goal.
