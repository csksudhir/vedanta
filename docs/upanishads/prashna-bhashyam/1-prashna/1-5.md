# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೫
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 5

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ಆದಿತ್ಯೋ ಹ ವೈ ಪ್ರಾಣೋ ರಯಿರೇವ ಚಂದ್ರಮಾ ರಯಿರ್ವಾ ಏತತ್ಸರ್ವಂ ಯನ್ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚಾಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ ತಸ್ಮಾನ್ಮೂರ್ತಿರೇವ ರಯಿಃ ॥ ೫ ॥

### Overall Translation

The Sun (Āditya) is indeed Prāṇa; the Moon (Candra) is indeed Rayi. Rayi is
indeed all this — whatever is formed (mūrta) and whatever is formless (amūrta).
Therefore the formed (mūrti) alone is Rayi.

### Word-to-Word

ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ — the Sun · ಹ ವೈ — indeed / verily · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — Prāṇa (the life-force / eater) · ರಯಿಃ ಏವ — Rayi alone / indeed Rayi · ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ — the Moon · ರಯಿಃ ವಾ — Rayi indeed · ಏತತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ — all this · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ — is formed (has form) and · ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ — is formless · ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ — therefore · ಮೂರ್ತಿಃ ಏವ — the formed alone · ರಯಿಃ — is Rayi ॥

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ತತ್ರ ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ ಹ ವೈ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ।
>
> ರಯಿಃ ಏವ ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ ।
>
> ರಯಿಃ ಏವ ಅನ್ನಂ ಸೋಮ ಏವ ।
>
> ತತ್ ಏತತ್ ಏಕಂ ಅತ್ತಾ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ ಅನ್ನಂ ಚ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಏಕಂ ತು ಮಿಥುನಮ್ ; ಗುಣಪ್ರಧಾನಕೃತಃ ಭೇದಃ ।
>
> ಕಥಮ್ ? ರಯಿಃ ವೈ ಅನ್ನಂ ಏವ ಏತತ್ ಸರ್ವಮ್ ।
>
> ಕಿಂ ತತ್ ? ಯತ್ ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ ಸ್ಥೂಲಂ ಚ ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ ಸೂಕ್ಷ್ಮಂ ಚ ।
>
> ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತೇ ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನರೂಪೇ ಅಪಿ ರಯಿಃ ಏವ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ಪ್ರವಿಭಕ್ತಾತ್ ಅಮೂರ್ತಾತ್ ಯತ್ ಅನ್ಯತ್ ಮೂರ್ತರೂಪಂ ಮೂರ್ತಿಃ, ಸಾ ಏವ ರಯಿಃ ಅನ್ನಂ ಅಮೂರ್ತೇನ ಅತ್ತ್ರಾ ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ ॥

### Overall Translation

There, in the matter of the second Praśna — the Sun (Āditya) is indeed Prāṇa,
the eater, the fire. The Moon alone is Rayi. Rayi alone is food, and that is
the Soma (the Moon) alone.

That this (Prajāpati) is one — being both the eater-fire and the food — is one
pair; and the distinction (between eater and food) is made by (one being)
primary and (the other being) subordinate in quality (guṇapradhānakṛta).

How (is all this Rayi)? Rayi — food — is indeed all this. What is that? Whatever
is formed (gross) and whatever is formless (subtle).

Both the formed and the formless — even in the roles of eater and food — are
Rayi alone.

Therefore, distinguished from the formless, whatever is other and of the nature
of form — that formed one (mūrti) alone is Rayi (food), because it is being eaten
by the formless eater (the formless Prāṇa).

### Word-to-Word

ತತ್ρ — there / in this (context of the second Praśna) · ಆದಿತ್ಯಃ ಹ ವೈ — the Sun indeed · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — Prāṇa · ಅತ್ತಾ — the eater · ಅಗ್ನಿಃ — Fire ।

ರಯಿಃ ಏವ — Rayi alone · ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ — the Moon ।

ರಯಿಃ ಏವ — Rayi alone · ಅನ್ನಂ — food · ಸೋಮಃ ಏವ — the Soma (Moon) alone ।

ತತ್ ಏತತ್ — that this · ಏಕಂ — one (entity) · ಅತ್ತಾ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ — the eater-fire and · ಅನ್ನಂ ಚ — food and · ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ — Prajāpati · ಏಕಂ ತು ಮಿಥುನಂ — one pair · ಗುಣಪ್ರಧಾನಕೃತಃ — made by subordinate and primary quality · ಭೇದಃ — the distinction ।

ಕಥಮ್ — how? · ರಯಿಃ ವೈ — Rayi indeed · ಅನ್ನಂ ಏವ — food alone · ಏತತ್ ಸರ್ವಂ — all this ।

ಕಿಂ ತತ್ — what is that? · ಯತ್ — whatever · ಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ — is formed and · ಸ್ಥೂಲಂ ಚ — is gross and · ಅಮೂರ್ತಂ ಚ — is formless and · ಸೂಕ್ಷ್ಮಂ ಚ — is subtle ।

ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತೇ — the formed and the formless · ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನರೂಪೇ ಅಪಿ — even in the roles of eater and food · ರಯಿಃ ಏವ — are Rayi alone ।

ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ — therefore · ಪ್ರವಿಭಕ್ತಾತ್ — distinguished from · ಅಮೂರ್ತಾತ್ — the formless · ಯತ್ ಅನ್ಯತ್ — whatever is other · ಮೂರ್ತರೂಪಂ — of the nature of form · ಮೂರ್ತಿಃ — the formed one · ಸಾ ಏವ ರಯಿಃ — that alone is Rayi · ಅನ್ನಂ — food · ಅಮೂರ್ತೇನ ಅತ್ತ್ರಾ — by the formless eater · ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ — because of being eaten ॥

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

Two sentences in this Bhāṣya require explicit anvaya — the central logical
statement about guṇapradhāna and the concluding resolution.

---

**Sentence 1 — The one pair and the guṇapradhāna distinction**

*As written:*
> ತತ್ ಏತತ್ ಏಕಂ ಅತ್ತಾ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ ಅನ್ನಂ ಚ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಏಕಂ ತು ಮಿಥುನಮ್ ;
> ಗುಣಪ್ರಧಾನಕೃತಃ ಭೇದಃ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> ತತ್ ಏತತ್ [ಅಗ್ನಿ-ಸೋಮ-ರೂಪಂ] ಏಕಂ [ಏವ] —
> [ಯತ್] ಅತ್ತಾ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ ಅನ್ನಂ ಚ [ತತ್] ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ [ಏಕ ಏವ] ।
>
> [ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ] ಏಕಂ ತು ಮಿಥುನಂ [ಭವತಿ] ।
>
> [ಅತ್ತಾ-ಅನ್ನಯೋಃ] ಭೇದಃ [ಕೃತಃ —] ಗುಣಪ್ರಧಾನ-ಕೃತಃ [ಕೇವಲಂ] ।

*English:* That this — of the nature of Fire and Moon — is one: what is both the
eater-fire and the food is Prajāpati alone. Prajāpati is one pair. The distinction
(between eater and food) is made solely by one being primary (pradhāna) and the
other subordinate in quality (guṇa).

*Note:* The key philosophical point is that the eater (attā) and the food (anna)
are not ontologically two — they are one Prajāpati. The distinction is
epistemological / functional: one aspect is primary (pradhāna — the eater, Prāṇa,
Fire) and the other is subordinate (guṇa — the food, Rayi, Moon). Ānandagiri
names this structure explicitly.

---

**Sentence 2 — Why the formed alone is Rayi when both formed and formless are Rayi**

*As written:*
> ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತೇ ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನರೂಪೇ ಅಪಿ ರಯಿಃ ಏವ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ಪ್ರವಿಭಕ್ತಾತ್ ಅಮೂರ್ತಾತ್ ಯತ್ ಅನ್ಯತ್ ಮೂರ್ತರೂಪಂ ಮೂರ್ತಿಃ,
> ಸಾ ಏವ ರಯಿಃ ಅನ್ನಂ ಅಮೂರ್ತೇನ ಅತ್ತ್ರಾ ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> [ಸಾಮಾನ್ಯತಃ] ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತೇ ಉಭೇ ಅಪಿ ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನ-ರೂಪೇ — ರಯಿಃ ಏವ [ಇತಿ ಉಕ್ತಂ] ।
>
> [ಕಿಂ ತು] ಯದಾ ವಿಭಕ್ತ-ದೃಷ್ಟ್ಯಾ —
> ಅಮೂರ್ತಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ [ಪ್ರಾಧಾನ್ಯೇನ ಅತ್ತಾ] —
> [ತದಾ] ಮೂರ್ತಿಃ [ಯಾ ಮೂರ್ತ-ರೂಪಂ] ಸಾ ಏವ ರಯಿಃ [ಭವತಿ] —
> [ಯತಃ] ಅಮೂರ್ತೇನ ಅತ್ತ್ರಾ ಅದ್ಯಮಾನಾ [ಸ್ತಿ] ।

*English:* In general — both the formed and the formless, even in the roles of
eater and food, are Rayi alone. But when they are viewed as distinguished from
each other — the formless Prāṇa being primarily the eater — then the formed one
(mūrti) alone is Rayi, because it is being eaten by the formless eater.

*Note:* This resolves the apparent contradiction in the Mūla: "all this —
both formed and formless — is Rayi," and yet "the formed alone is Rayi."
Śaṅkara explains it as two levels of description — the undivided view
(where everything is Rayi) and the distinguished view (where only the formed
is Rayi, being eaten by the formless).

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣೌ ಶ್ರುತಿಃ ಸ್ವಯಂ ಏವ ವ್ಯಾಚಷ್ಟ ಇತಿ ಆಹ – ತತ್ರ ಆದಿತ್ಯ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಜಾಪತೇಃ ಏವ ಸಂವತ್ಸರ-ಆದಿ-ಪ್ರಜಾ-ಪರ್ಯಂತ-ಸ್ರಷ್ಟೃತ್ವಂ ವಕ್ತುಂ ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣಯೋಃ ಸಂವತ್ಸರ-ಸ್ರಷ್ಟ್ರೋಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯ-ಉಪಾದಾನತ್ವಾತ್ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮತ್ವಂ ಆಹ – ತದೇತದೇಕಂ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಕಥಂ ಏಕಸ್ಯ ಅತ್ತಾ ಅನ್ನಂ ಚ ಇತಿ ಭೇದಃ ಇತ್ಯಾಶಂಕ್ಯ ತಸ್ಯ ಏವ ಗುಣಭಾವ-ವಿವಕ್ಷಯಾ ಅನ್ನತ್ವಂ ಪ್ರಾಧಾನ್ಯ-ವಿವಕ್ಷಯಾ ಚ ಅತ್ತೃತ್ವಂ ಇತಿ ಭೇದಃ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ಗುಣ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ರಯಿಪ್ರಾಣಯೋಃ ಕಥಂ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತ್ಯಾತ್ಮತ್ವಂ ಇತಿ ಶಂಕತೇ — ಕಥಂ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ತತ್ρ ರಯೇಃ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಕತ್ವಾತ್ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿತ್ವಂ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ರಯಿಃ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಅಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ ಅಪಿ ವಾಯ್ವಾದೇಃ ಕೇನಚಿತ್ ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ ರಯಿತ್ವಂ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ನನು ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತಯೋಃ ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನಯೋಃ ಉಭಯೋಃ ಅಪಿ ರಯಿತ್ವೇ ಅನ್ನಂ ಏವ ರಯಿಃ ಇತಿ ಕಥಂ ಉಕ್ತಂ ಇತ್ಯಾಶಂಕ್ಯ ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತತ್ವ-ವಿಭಾಗಂ ಅಕೃತ್ವಾ ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ ಗುಣಭಾವ-ಮಾತ್ರ-ವಿವಕ್ಷಯಾ ಸರ್ವಂ ರಯಿಃ ಇತ್ಯುಚ್ಯತೇ ।
>
> ಯದಾ ಉಭೇ ವಿಭಜ್ಯ ಗುಣ-ಪ್ರಧಾನ-ಭಾವೇನ ವಿವಕ್ಷ್ಯೇತೇ ತದಾ ಅಮೂರ್ತೇನ ಪ್ರಾಣೇನ ಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ ಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ ಏವ ರಯಿತ್ವಂ ಇತ್ಯಾಹ – ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ಇತಿ ॥ ೫ ॥

### Overall Translation

Ānandagiri opens by noting that Śaṅkara states the Śruti itself explains Rayi
and Prāṇa — with the words "tatra āditya" (there, the Sun). 

To establish that Prajāpati alone is the creator from the Year down to creatures,
Śaṅkara states the Prajāpati-nature of Rayi and Prāṇa — since the two, who are
the creators of the Year, have Prajāpati as their material cause (upādāna). This
is done with the words "tad etad ekam" (that this is one).

Ānandagiri then raises and resolves the objection: how can one (Prajāpati) have
the distinction of being both eater and food? The answer is: its food-nature is
stated with the intent of describing a subordinate aspect (guṇabhāva-vivakṣā),
and its eater-nature is stated with the intent of describing the primary aspect
(prādhānya-vivakṣā). This is what Śaṅkara means by "guṇa" (subordinate quality).

A further objection is raised: how can Rayi and Prāṇa be of the nature of
Prajāpati? — with the word "katham" (how?).

Śaṅkara's answer — that Rayi is Prajāpati because Rayi is the Self of all (sarvātmaka)
— is confirmed. The implication is: even the formless — such as air (vāyu) and
the like — has the quality of Rayi (food-nature) because it is eaten by something.

Ānandagiri then raises and resolves another objection: if both the formed and
the formless (in the roles of eater and food) are Rayi, how was it said that
"food alone is Rayi"? The answer: without making the distinction of formed and
formless, with the intent of describing everything as having only the subordinate
quality (guṇabhāvamātra) of food-nature, all is said to be Rayi.

But when the two are distinguished and described in terms of primary and
subordinate — then, because the formed is eaten by the formless Prāṇa, only
the formed has the Rayi-nature — with the word "tasmāt" (therefore).

ಅಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ ಅಪಿ — even of the formless · ವಾಯ್ವಾದೇಃ — such as air (vāyu) and the like · ಕೇನಚಿತ್ — by something · ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ — because of being eaten · ರಯಿತ್ವಂ — the Rayi-nature (food-nature) · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

ನನು — now / but (objection) · ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತಯೋಃ — of both the formed and the formless · ಅತ್ರ-ಅನ್ನಯೋಃ — in the roles of eater and food · ಉಭಯೋಃ ಅಪಿ — both · ರಯಿತ್ವೇ — having Rayi-nature · ಅನ್ನಂ ಏವ ರಯಿಃ — food alone is Rayi · ಇತಿ ಕಥಂ ಉಕ್ತಂ — how was this said · ಇತ್ಯಾಶಂಕ್ಯ — anticipating this objection · ಮೂರ್ತ-ಅಮೂರ್ತತ್ವ-ವಿಭಾಗಂ ಅಕೃತ್ವಾ — without making the distinction of formed and formless · ಸರ್ವಸ್ಯ — of all · ಗುಣಭಾವ-ಮಾತ್ರ-ವಿವಕ್ಷಯಾ — with the intent of describing only the subordinate quality · ಸರ್ವಂ ರಯಿಃ — all is Rayi · ಇತ್ಯುಚ್ಯತೇ — it is said ।

ಯದಾ — when · ಉಭೇ — both · ವಿಭಜ್ಯ — having distinguished · ಗುಣ-ಪ್ರಧಾನ-ಭಾವೇನ — in terms of subordinate and primary · ವಿವಕ್ಷ್ಯೇತೇ — are to be described · ತದಾ — then · ಅಮೂರ್ತೇನ ಪ್ರಾಣೇನ — by the formless Prāṇa · ಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ — of the formed · ಅದ್ಯಮಾನತ್ವಾತ್ — because of being eaten · ಮೂರ್ತಸ್ಯ ಏವ ರಯಿತ್ವಂ — the Rayi-nature belongs to the formed alone · ಇತ್ಯಾಹ — states · ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ಇತಿ — with the word "tasmāt (therefore)" ॥

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | Identifies Sun as Prāṇa and Moon as Rayi; states all — both formed and formless — is Rayi; then qualifies: the formed alone is Rayi |
| **ಭಾṣya** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Shows Sun = Prāṇa = Fire = eater; Moon = Rayi = food = Soma; explains the one Prajāpati as a pair (mithuna) with the distinction made by guṇa-pradhāna; resolves the apparent contradiction between "all is Rayi" and "the formed alone is Rayi" |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | Notes the Śruti explains Rayi-Prāṇa itself; explains Prajāpati-upādānatva of Rayi-Prāṇa; resolves the one-but-two objection via guṇabhāva-vivakṣā / prādhānya-vivakṣā; distinguishes two levels of description — undivided (all is Rayi) and divided (formed alone is Rayi) |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **Sun = Prāṇa = Fire = eater; Moon = Rayi = food = Soma.** This is the
   foundational identification of the second Praśna, stated here without
   qualification.
2. **Prajāpati is one pair (ekaṃ mithuna)** — the eater and the food are not
   two separate entities but one Prajāpati viewed from two aspects. The
   distinction is guṇapradhānakṛta — made by one being primary, the other
   subordinate.
3. **Two levels of description** resolve the apparent contradiction in the Mūla:
   At the undivided level (guṇabhāvamātra-vivakṣā) — all, both formed and
   formless, is Rayi. At the divided level (guṇapradhānabhāva-vivakṣā) — only
   the formed is Rayi, because it is eaten by the formless.
4. **The formless too has Rayi-nature** — even air (vāyu) and other formless
   principles are eaten by something and are therefore Rayi from one perspective.
   This is Ānandagiri's important clarification on the scope of Rayi.
5. **Prajāpati-upādānatva** — Prajāpati is the material cause (upādāna) of
   Rayi and Prāṇa. Since they are created from Prajāpati and are of the nature
   of Prajāpati, their being the Prajāpati-natured pair (mithuna) is established.
