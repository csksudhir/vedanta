# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೧೩
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 13

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ಅಹೋರಾತ್ರೋ ವೈ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಸ್ತಸ್ಯಾಹರೇವ ಪ್ರಾಣೋ ರಾತ್ರಿರೇವ ರಯಿಃ ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ವಾ ಏತೇ ಪ್ರಸ್ಕಂದಂತಿ ಯೇ ದಿವಾ ರತ್ಯಾ ಸಂಯುಜ್ಯಂತೇ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯಮೇವ ತದ್ಯದ್ರಾತ್ರೌ ರತ್ಯಾ ಸಂಯುಜ್ಯಂತೇ ॥ ೧೩ ॥

### Overall Translation

The Day-and-Night (ahorātra) is indeed Prajāpati. Of it, the day (ahas) alone is
Prāṇa and the night (rātri) alone is Rayi. Those who unite with a woman by day
indeed waste / lose their Prāṇa. That which is uniting with a woman by night is
brahmacarya (celibacy / proper conduct) alone.

### Word-to-Word

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ಸೋಽಪಿ ಮಾಸಾತ್ಮಾ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಸ್ವಾವಯವೇ ಅಹೋರಾತ್ರೇ ಪರಿಸಮಾಪ್ಯತೇ ।
>
> ಅಹೋರಾತ್ರೋ ವೈ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿಃ ಪೂರ್ವವತ್ ।
>
> ತಸ್ಯಾಪಿ ಅಹರೇವ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ರಾತ್ರಿರೇವ ರಯಿಃ ಪೂರ್ವವದೇವ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಾಣಮ್ ಅಹರಾತ್ಮಾನಂ ವೈ ಏತೇ ಪ್ರಸ್ಕಂದಂತಿ ನಿರ್ಗಮಯಂತಿ ಶೋಷಯಂತಿ ವಾ ಸ್ವಾತ್ಮನೋ ವಿಚ್ಛಿದ್ಯಾಪನಯಂತಿ ।
>
> ಕೇ ? ಯೇ ದಿವಾ ಅಹನಿ ರತ್ಯಾ ರತಿಕಾರಣಭೂತಯಾ ಸಹ ಸ್ತ್ರಿಯಾ ಸಂಯುಜ್ಯಂತೇ ಮೈಥುನಮಾಚರಂತಿ ಮೂಢಾಃ ।
>
> ಯತ ಏವಂ ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ತನ್ನ ಕರ್ತವ್ಯಮಿತಿ ಪ್ರತಿಷೇಧಃ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕಃ ।
>
> ಯತ್ ರಾತ್ರೌ ಸಂಯುಜ್ಯಂತೇ ರತ್ಯಾ ಋತೌ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯಮೇವ ತದಿತಿ ಪ್ರಶಸ್ತತ್ವಾತ್ ರಾತ್ರೌ ಭಾರ್ಯಾಗಮನಂ ಕರ್ತವ್ಯಮಿತ್ಯಯಮಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕೋ ವಿಧಿಃ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಕೃತಂ ತೂಚ್ಯತೇ ಸೋಽಹೋರಾತ್ರಾತ್ಮಕಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಪತಿರ್ವ್ರೀಹಿಯವಾದ್ಯನ್ನಾತ್ಮನಾ ವ್ಯವಸ್ಥಿತಃ ॥

### Overall Translation

That Prajāpati whose nature is the Month is also completely contained within his
own limb, the Day-and-Night (ahorātra).

The Day-and-Night is indeed Prajāpati — as has been stated before (with the same
characteristics).

Of it too, the day alone is Prāṇa — the eater, the fire — and the night alone is
Rayi — exactly as before.

Those who are foolish — they indeed lose / waste / drain away the Prāṇa whose
nature is the day; they cause it to flow out, to dry up, or they cut it away and
remove it from themselves.

Who are they? Those fools who by day unite with a woman — whose purpose is
enjoyment — and engage in intercourse.

Since this is so, therefore this must not be done — this prohibition is incidental
(prāsaṅgika — arising from the context, not the primary topic).

That which is uniting with a woman by night in the proper season (ṛtu) is
brahmacarya alone — because it is praiseworthy; therefore the injunction that
approaching one's wife by night in the proper season is to be done — this too is
an incidental injunction (prāsaṅgika vidhi).

The main topic, however, is now stated: that Prajāpati who is of the nature of the
Day-and-Night is established as food — in the form of rice, barley, and the like.

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

One sentence in this Bhāṣya requires explicit anvaya treatment.

---

**Sentence — The incidental prohibition and the incidental injunction**

*As written:*
> ಯತ ಏವಂ ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ತನ್ನ ಕರ್ತವ್ಯಮಿತಿ ಪ್ರತಿಷೇಧಃ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕಃ ।
>
> ಯತ್ ರಾತ್ರೌ ಸಂಯುಜ್ಯಂತೇ ರತ್ಯಾ ಋತೌ ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯಮೇವ ತದಿತಿ ಪ್ರಶಸ್ತತ್ವಾತ್
> ರಾತ್ರೌ ಭಾರ್ಯಾಗಮನಂ ಕರ್ತವ್ಯಮಿತ್ಯಯಮಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕೋ ವಿಧಿಃ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> [ದಿವಾ ಮೈಥುನಂ ಪ್ರಾಣಕ್ಷಯಕರಮ್] ಇತಿ ಯತಃ ಏವಂ [ಉಕ್ತಂ] —
> ತಸ್ಮಾತ್ ತತ್ ನ ಕರ್ತವ್ಯಂ [ಇತ್ಯಾಯಂ] ಪ್ರತಿಷೇಧಃ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕಃ [ಭವತಿ] ।
>
> [ಅಪರಃ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕಃ] —
> ಯತ್ ರಾತ್ರೌ ಋತೌ ರತ್ಯಾ ಸಂಯುಜ್ಯಂತೇ [ತತ್] ಬ್ರಹ್ಮಚರ್ಯಂ ಏವ [ಭವತಿ] ।
>
> [ತಸ್ಯ] ಪ್ರಶಸ್ತತ್ವಾತ್ ರಾತ್ರೌ [ಋತೌ] ಭಾರ್ಯಾಗಮನಂ ಕರ್ತವ್ಯಂ —
> ಇತಿ ಅಯಂ ಅಪಿ ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕಃ ವಿಧಿಃ [ಭವತಿ] ।

*English:* Because intercourse by day causes loss of Prāṇa — therefore this must
not be done: this prohibition is incidental (to the main topic). And conversely —
that which is uniting with a woman by night in the proper season is brahmacarya
alone; because of its being praiseworthy, the injunction that approaching one's
wife by night in the proper season is to be done — this too is an incidental
injunction.

*Note:* Śaṅkara uses the technical term ಪ್ರಾಸಂಗಿಕ (prāsaṅgika — incidental /
arising from context) twice — once for the prohibition (pratiṣedha) and once for
the positive injunction (vidhi). This signals that neither daytime abstinence nor
nighttime union is the primary subject of the verse — the primary subject (ಪ್ರಕೃತ)
is Prajāpati as food. Ānandagiri confirms this explicitly in the Ṭīkā.

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ರಯಿಃ ಪೂರ್ವವತ್ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ರಯಿಃ ಅನ್ನಂ ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಅಹ್ನಃ ಪ್ರಾಣತ್ವ-ಉಕ್ತಿ-ಪ್ರಸಂಗಾತ್ ಅಹ್ನಿ ಮೈಥುನಂ ನಿಷೇಧತಿ – ಪ್ರಾಣಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಪ್ರಕೃತಮಿತಿ ।
>
> ಕುತೋ ಹ ವಾ ಇಮಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತೇ ಇತಿ ಪೃಷ್ಟಂ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ಪೂರ್ವೋಕ್ತಂ ಸರ್ವಂ ಏತತ್ ಉಪಯೋಗಿತಯಾ ಉಕ್ತಂ ನ ತು ಸಾಕ್ಷಾತ್ ಪ್ರಕೃತಂ ಇತಿ ಭಾವಃ ॥ ೧೩ ॥

### Overall Translation

On "rāyiḥ pūrvavat" (Rayi as before): Ānandagiri explains this means Rayi is
food and the Moon — i.e., the same identification as established in the earlier
analysis of the Year and Month.

On the prohibition of daytime intercourse: Ānandagiri explains that by occasion
of (ＵKti-prasaṅga — the incidental context of) stating the day's Prāṇa-nature,
Śaṅkara prohibits intercourse by day — with the words "prāṇam" (they lose Prāṇa).
This is a prāsaṅgika (incidental) prohibition arising from the context, not the
primary teaching.

On "prakṛtam" (the main topic): Ānandagiri clarifies that the primary subject
(prakṛta) is what was originally asked — "from where indeed are these creatures
born?" — i.e., the question put to the sage at the beginning of the first Praśna.
The import is: everything stated above — the Year, Month, fortnight, Day-and-Night
— was all stated as being useful (upayogita) for answering that question, but none
of it was the direct primary topic (sākṣāt prakṛta). The primary topic now returns:
Prajāpati established as food in the form of rice, barley, and the like — from
which creatures are born.

### Word-to-Word

ರಯಿಃ ಪೂರ್ವವತ್ ಇತಿ — on the phrase "Rayi as before" ।

ರಯಿಃ — Rayi · ಅನ್ನಂ — food · ಚಂದ್ರಮಾಃ — the Moon · ಇತಿ ಅರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

ಅಹ್ನಃ — of the day · ಪ್ರಾಣತ್ವ-ಉಕ್ತಿ-ಪ್ರಸಂಗಾತ್ — by occasion of / incidentally from the statement of the day's Prāṇa-nature · ಅಹ್ನಿ — by day / in the daytime · ಮೈಥುನಂ — intercourse · ಣಿಷೇಧತಿ — prohibits · ಪ್ರಾಣಂ ಇತಿ — with the word "prāṇam (Prāṇa)" ।

ಪ್ರಕೃತಂ ಇತಿ — on the word "prakṛtam (the main topic)" ।

ಕುತಃ ಹ ವಾ ಇಮಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಃ ಪ್ರಜಾಯಂತೇ — "from where indeed are these creatures born?" · ಇತಿ ಪೃಷ್ಟಂ — thus what was asked (at the beginning) · ಇತಿ ಅರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

ಪೂರ್ವೋಕ್ತಂ ಸರ್ವಂ — everything stated above · ಏತತ್ — this · ಉಪಯೋಗಿತಯಾ — as being useful / as a means · ಉಕ್ತಂ — was stated · ನ ತು — but not · ಸಾಕ್ಷಾತ್ ಪ್ರಕೃತಂ — the direct primary topic · ಇತಿ ಭಾವಃ — this is the import ॥

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | Applies the Rayi-Prāṇa analysis to the Day-and-Night; night is Rayi, day is Prāṇa; daytime intercourse wastes Prāṇa; nighttime union in season is brahmacarya |
| **ಭಾಷ್ಯ** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Continues the fractal descent (Year → Month → Day-and-Night); identifies the prohibition and injunction as prāsaṅgika (incidental); identifies the primary topic as Prajāpati as food (rice, barley etc.) from which creatures are born |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | Clarifies Rayi = food = Moon; explains the prohibition as arising from the incidental occasion of stating the day's Prāṇa-nature; most importantly identifies "prakṛtam" as pointing back to the original question of the Praśna — the primary topic is how creatures are born, and everything from the Year onwards was preparatory context (upayogita) for that answer |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **The fractal descent is complete** — Year → Month → Fortnight → Day-and-Night.
   At every level, Rayi and Prāṇa are the two aspects of Prajāpati.
2. **Day = Prāṇa = fire = eater; Night = Rayi = Moon = food.** The daytime is the
   consuming, active principle; the night is the nourishing, material principle.
3. **The prohibition and injunction are prāsaṅgika** — incidental, not primary.
   Śaṅkara is careful to distinguish what is directly relevant to the main topic
   from what arises as a contextual aside.
4. **The primary topic (prakṛtam) now returns** — the original question of the
   Praśna: from what are creatures born? Prajāpati as food (rice, barley etc.) is
   the answer. Everything from the Year analysis was upayogita — useful context,
   not the direct answer.
5. **Ānandagiri's key contribution** is pinpointing "prakṛtam" as the structural
   signal that Śaṅkara is now returning to the original question after the long
   Rayi-Prāṇa analysis — tying the entire philosophical excursus back to its
   starting point.
