# ಪ್ರಶ್ನೋಪನಿಷತ್ — ಪ್ರಥಮ ಪ್ರಶ್ನ, ಮಂತ್ರ ೭
# Praśna Upaniṣad — First Question, Verse 7

> **Source of Ṭīkā:** Ānandāśrama Sanskrit Series No. 8 (Ānandagiri on Śaṅkara Bhāṣya)

---

## ಮೂಲ (Mūla)

> ಸ ಏಷ ವೈಶ್ವಾನರೋ ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ ಪ್ರಾಣೋಽಗ್ನಿರುದಯತೇ ।
>
> ತದೇತದೃಚಾಭ್ಯುಕ್ತಮ್ ॥ ೭ ॥

### Overall Translation

This very one — the Vaiśvānara, of universal form, Prāṇa, Fire — rises.

And this same matter has been stated (confirmed) by a Ṛc (Vedic verse) as well.

### Word-to-Word

ಸಃ ಏಷಃ — this very one / that same one · ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ — the Vaiśvānara (the fire common to all living beings) · ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ — of universal form · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — Prāṇa (the life-force) · ಅಗ್ನಿಃ — Fire · ಉದಯತೇ — rises ।

ತತ್ ಏತತ್ — that this (matter) · ಋಚಾ — by a Ṛc (Vedic verse) · ಅಭ್ಯುಕ್ತಂ — has been stated / confirmed ॥

---

## ಭಾಷ್ಯ (Bhāṣya)

> ಸ ಏಷಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ ವಿಶ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಾಚ್ಚ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ ಸ ಏವ ಅತ್ತಾ ಉದಯತೇ ಉದ್ಗಚ್ಛತಿ ಪ್ರತ್ಯಹಂ ಸರ್ವಾ ದಿಶಃ ಆತ್ಮಸಾತ್ಕುರ್ವನ್ ।
>
> ತದೇತತ್ ಉಕ್ತಂ ವಸ್ತು ಋಚಾ ಮಂತ್ರೇಣ ಅಪಿ ಅಭ್ಯುಕ್ತಂ ॥

### Overall Translation

This very one — the eater, Prāṇa — is the Vaiśvānara, the Self of all;
and of universal form because he is the Self of the universe. And this same
one — Prāṇa, Fire, and the eater — rises, ascends, every day, making all
directions his own Self.

And this matter that has been stated (the greatness of the Sun / Āditya) has
also been confirmed by a Ṛc — by a mantra.

### Word-to-Word

ಸಃ ಏಷಃ — this very one · ಅತ್ತಾ — the eater / consumer · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — Prāṇa · ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ — the Vaiśvānara · ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ — the Self of all · ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ — of universal form · ವಿಶ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಾತ್ ಚ — and because of being the Self of the universe · ಪ್ರಾಣಃ — Prāṇa · ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ — and Fire · ಸ ಏವ — that very one · ಅತ್ತಾ — the eater · ಉದಯತೇ — rises · ಉದ್ಗಚ್ಛತಿ — ascends · ಪ್ರತ್ಯಹಂ — every day · ಸರ್ವಾ ದಿಶಃ — all directions · ಆತ್ಮಸಾತ್ಕುರ್ವನ್ — making (them) his own Self ।

ತತ್ ಏತತ್ — that this · ಉಕ್ತಂ ವಸ್ತು — the matter that has been stated · ಋಚಾ — by a Ṛc · ಮಂತ್ರೇಣ ಅಪಿ — also by a mantra · ಅಭ್ಯುಕ್ತಂ — has been confirmed / further stated ॥

---

### Anvaya (Prose Order of the Bhāṣya)

The key sentence in this Bhāṣya accumulates five predicative identifications
of the same subject — requiring explicit anvaya to show they all refer to one
entity.

---

**Sentence — The fivefold identification of the rising Prāṇa**

*As written:*
> ಸ ಏಷಃ ಅತ್ತಾ ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ ವಿಶ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಾಚ್ಚ
> ಪ್ರಾಣಃ ಅಗ್ನಿಃ ಚ ಸ ಏವ ಅತ್ತಾ ಉದಯತೇ ಪ್ರತ್ಯಹಂ ಸರ್ವಾ ದಿಶಃ ಆತ್ಮಸಾತ್ಕುರ್ವನ್ ।

*Anvaya (resolved prose order):*
> ಸಃ ಏಷಃ [ಏಕ ಏವ ಸ್ವಯಂ] —
> ಅತ್ತಾ [ಚ], ಪ್ರಾಣಃ [ಚ], ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ [ಚ], ಸರ್ವಾತ್ಮಾ [ಚ],
> ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ [ಚ — ವಿಶ್ವಾತ್ಮತ್ವಾತ್], ಅಗ್ನಿಃ [ಚ] —
> ಸಃ ಏವ ಅತ್ತಾ ಪ್ರತ್ಯಹಂ ಸರ್ವಾ ದಿಶಃ ಆತ್ಮಸಾತ್ಕುರ್ವನ್ ಉದಯತೇ ।

*English:* That very one — who is simultaneously the eater, Prāṇa, Vaiśvānara,
the Self of all, of universal form (because he is the Self of the universe), and
Fire — that very eater rises every day, making all directions his own Self.

*Note:* Śaṅkara uses "sa evaita" twice — first to introduce the cluster of
identifications and then to resume the subject before the finite verb ಉದಯತೇ.
The participial ಆತ್ಮಸಾತ್ಕುರ್ವನ್ (making his own Self) is the key phrase —
Ānandagiri's gloss on "pratyakṣatvam" (perceptibility / directness) confirms
this rising is the observable, daily manifestation of the one Prāṇa-Fire-Sun.

---

## ಟೀಕಾ (Ṭīkā)

> ತಸ್ಯ ಪ್ರತ್ಯಕ್ಷತ್ವಮಾಹ – ಸ ಏಷ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ವೈಶ್ವಾನರ ಇತಿ ।
>
> ನರಾ ಜೀವಾ ವಿಶ್ವೇ ಚ ತೇ ನರಾಃ ಚ ವಿಶ್ವಾನರಾಃ ಸ ಏವ ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ ಸರ್ವಜೀವಾತ್ಮಕ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ।
>
> ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಪಂಚಾತ್ಮಕ ಇತಿ ಭೇದಃ ।
>
> ಉಕ್ತಂ ವಸ್ತು ಇತಿ ।
>
> ಆದಿತ್ಯಸ್ಯ ಉಕ್ತಂ ಮಾಹಾತ್ಮ್ಯಂ ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ ॥ ೭ ॥

### Overall Translation

Ānandagiri opens by noting that Śaṅkara states the perceptibility (pratyakṣatva)
of that Prāṇa — with the words "sa eṣa" (this very one). The point is that what
has been described philosophically throughout the second Praśna is now directly
identified with the observable daily rising of the Sun.

On "vaiśvānara": Ānandagiri gives the etymological derivation. The nāras (living
beings / jīvas) are all — they are the viśve nārāḥ (all the living beings); that
which belongs to or pervades all the living beings is vaiśvānara. Therefore
vaiśvānara means "of the nature of all living beings / the Self of all jīvas."

Ānandagiri then distinguishes vaiśvānara from viśvarūpa: vaiśvānara means the
Self of all living beings (sarvajīvātmaka — the jīva aspect), whereas viśvarūpa
means the Self of the entire manifest world / all phenomena (sarvaprapañcātmaka
— the prapañca / universe aspect). These are two distinct aspects of the same
Prāṇa-Sun.

On "uktaṃ vastu" (the matter that has been stated): Ānandagiri clarifies this
means the greatness of the Sun (Āditya) that has been described in the preceding
verses of the second Praśna — and this greatness is now being further confirmed
by a Ṛc (Vedic verse, which follows in verse 8).

### Word-to-Word

ತಸ್ಯ — of that (Prāṇa-Sun) · ಪ್ರತ್ಯಕ್ಷತ್ವಂ — the perceptibility / directness / observable nature · ಆಹ — states · ಸ ಏಷ ಇತಿ — with the words "sa eṣa (this very one)" ।

ವೈಶ್ವಾನರ ಇತಿ — on the word "vaiśvānara" ।

ನರಾಃ — living beings / jīvas · ಜೀವಾಃ — jīvas · ವಿಶ್ವೇ ಚ ತೇ ನರಾಃ ಚ — all of them together being the nāras · ವಿಶ್ವಾನರಾಃ — the viśvānara (all-pervading living beings) · ಸ ಏವ — that very one · ವೈಶ್ವಾನರಃ — is the Vaiśvānara · ಸರ್ವಜೀವಾತ್ಮಕಃ — of the nature of / being the Self of all living beings · ಇತಿ ಅರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ।

ವಿಶ್ವರೂಪಃ — viśvarūpa (of universal form) · ಸರ್ವಪ್ರಪಂಚಾತ್ಮಕಃ — being the Self of the entire manifest world / all phenomena · ಇತಿ ಭೇದಃ — this is the distinction (between vaiśvānara and viśvarūpa) ।

ಉಕ್ತಂ ವಸ್ತು ಇತಿ — on the phrase "uktaṃ vastu (the matter that has been stated)" ।

ಆದಿತ್ಯಸ್ಯ — of the Sun (Āditya) · ಉಕ್ತಂ ಮಾಹಾತ್ಮ್ಯಂ — the greatness that has been described · ಇತ್ಯರ್ಥಃ — this is the meaning ॥

## Summary of Mūla, Bhāṣya, and Ṭīkā

| Text | Author | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **ಮೂಲ** (Mūla) | Upaniṣad (Atharva-veda tradition) | Identifies the rising Sun with Vaiśvānara, Viśvarūpa, Prāṇa, and Fire — and announces that a Vedic verse (Ṛc) will confirm this |
| **ಭಾṣya** (Bhāṣya) | Śaṅkarācārya (8th c. CE) | Explicates the five identifications of the one Prāṇa-Sun (attā, prāṇa, vaiśvānara, sarvātmā, viśvarūpa, agni); identifies the daily rising as the observable manifestation of this one entity making all directions his own Self |
| **ಟೀಕಾ** (Ṭīkā) | Ānandagiri (13th c. CE) | States that Śaṅkara is establishing the pratyakṣatva (perceptibility) of Prāṇa; gives the etymology of vaiśvānara; distinguishes vaiśvānara (Self of all jīvas) from viśvarūpa (Self of all phenomena); identifies "uktaṃ vastu" as the greatness of Āditya described earlier |

### Core Philosophical Thread

1. **Verse 7 is the pivot** between the philosophical analysis of the second
   Praśna and the Vedic śloka that will confirm it in verse 8. Verse 7 draws
   the analysis to a perceptible, observable conclusion — the daily rising of
   the Sun.
2. **The five identifications** — attā (eater), prāṇa (life-force), vaiśvānara
   (Self of all jīvas), viśvarūpa (Self of all phenomena), agni (fire) — are
   all names of the same one Prāṇa-Sun. Śaṅkara's Bhāṣya gathers them all
   into a single subject.
3. **ಆತ್ಮಸಾತ್ಕುರ್ವನ್ (ātmasātkurvan)** — "making all directions his own Self"
   — is philosophically the most significant phrase. The Sun's rising is not
   merely physical motion but the Self asserting its identity with all
   directions, all space.
4. **Ānandagiri's vaiśvānara / viśvarūpa distinction** is precise and
   important: vaiśvānara = Self of all jīvas (the individual living beings);
   viśvarūpa = Self of the entire prapañca (the manifest universe as a whole).
   Both are aspects of the same Prāṇa-Sun — the jīva-aspect and the
   prapañca-aspect.
5. **The ṛc cited in verse 8** is now announced here — "tadetan ṛcābhyuktam"
   links verse 7 structurally to verse 8, making the two verses a unit: prose
   identification followed by Vedic verse confirmation.
